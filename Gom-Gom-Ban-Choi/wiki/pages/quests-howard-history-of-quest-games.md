# History of quests in games

Source: [[quests-howard-src]], Introduction pp. 10-19.

Howard's lineage (history/analysis, mostly Western PC/console RPG and adventure titles):
1. **Tabletop** (1974 *Dungeons & Dragons*, Gygax/Arneson; precursor *Chainmail*). "Quest" appears as a unit of play around which sessions are built; modules/scenarios are the paper form. Emigh's *The Quest* (a referee's design guide drawing on myth and romance) and Gygax's "Master's Library" show designers consciously using literary sources (pp. 11-12).
2. **Text interactive fiction**: Crowther's *Adventure* (1975), Woods's *Colossal Cave* (1977): collect treasures, solve puzzles. Niesz and Holland say the structure of IF is "the Quest"; Howard notes early IF was often scavenger hunt rather than quest, lacking a contextualised goal (pp. 12-13).
3. **Graphical adventure**: Roberta Williams's *King's Quest* (1983-98). Sequels add narrative coherence; Williams wants players to have "a clear sense of what you're doing and why, with some emotion behind it" instead of a jumble of meaningless puzzles (p. 14).
4. **Console action-adventure**: Atari 2600 *Adventure* (Robinett): a treasure hunt becomes a quest when each barrier needs an object, which needs another object, producing sub-goals (p. 15). *Legend of Zelda*: main quest broken into manageable parts; *Ocarina of Time* adds a quest-status screen that records progress via collected medallions (p. 15).
5. **First-generation CRPG**: mainframe *dnd* (kill the dragon, fetch the Orb), *Akalabeth*, *Ultima I-III* (dungeon crawls, fetch quests), *Wizardry* (fetch the amulet). *Ultima IV: Quest of the Avatar* adds a virtue system and meaning (pp. 16-17).
6. **Second-generation CRPG**: open worlds with many interrelated side quests tracked in a **journal** of tasks given by NPCs (*Baldur's Gate*, *Neverwinter Nights*, *Elder Scrolls*). Barton: the best CRPGs offer "far better rewards than just experience points and gold coins" (pp. 17-18). Meaningful titles (*Planescape: Torment*) are not always best sellers; hack-and-slash (*Diablo*) sells more (pp. 17-18).
7. **MMORPG** (*Ultima Online*, *EverQuest*, *World of Warcraft*): the single main quest fades; thousands of collaborative side-quest-like tasks, quest *lines*, group quests, "lore quests" (Kelly) (p. 19).

Pattern Howard draws: each lineage starts with chores/puzzles and moves toward quests whose actions *mean* something; meaning helps marketing and critical reputation more than raw first-release sales.

## Stance and applicability
(a) PC/console RPG, adventure and MMO history; mobile is out of frame (2008 book, pre-app-store). (b) Historical narrative from secondary sources and designer interviews; no data. (c) Compare [[f2pdive-bycer-history-and-three-generations]]: Howard's "chores to meaning" arc is a PC/console story, while mobile daily missions and battle-pass tasks descend from MMO daily quests that he barely mentions. His "proliferating tasks" in MMOs is the same treadmill criticised in [[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]] (grinding). (d) Fit for casual match-3 + deco: **low-medium**. Useful as precedent only: (i) the Atari *Adventure* observation (sub-goals chained by required objects) is the seed of quest chains; (ii) the *Zelda* quest-status screen is the ancestor of a milestone-quest progress view.
