# Low-stakes design rules (Bycer)

Source: [[cozydive-bycer-src]] ch. 2.2, 3.4 (energy penalty), 5.2, 5.4, 5.1.

## Rules he states
1. **No long-term consequences** for mistakes (5.2). Short-term penalty is acceptable and bounded. Documented example: Stardew Valley - out of energy or awake past 2 AM means passing out, less energy next day and some money lost; dying in the mines drops a few items recoverable for a fee; everything resets by next morning (3.4).
2. **Avoid loss states** in gameplay; reframe mechanics (undersea example, 4.2: same dive mechanic as horror vs infinite oxygen and no predators). Getting Over It is the anti-example (all progress can be lost). A Short Hike: no penalties at all.
3. **Freedom to change your mind**: include respec or free rebuilds (Cauldron, 5.2). Opinion: for cozy it is "a no-brainer". Still keep interesting choices; low stakes is not "dumbed down".
4. **Never force a clock**: avoid countdown timers that make the player decide fast; goals with a clearly communicated deadline are acceptable (5.2). Optional timed content only late for hardcore players (Stardew Ginger Island job board, 5.2).
5. **Make desired behaviour easy and unpunished** (5.2): if you want players to use an item, make it easy to get; hoarding comes from fear of not getting another.
6. **Skip/auto-solve when stuck** (5.4): Artifex Mundi hidden-object games run a timer on each puzzle and offer auto-solve when it ends; "if someone is stuck or frustrated... it is failing at being cozy".
7. **Assist modes**: Celeste lets players turn off challenge (5.2). Caveat (documented via achievements): Celeste still lost 30% before finishing chapter 1, so assist options do not replace UI/UX work (Fig. 5.4).
8. **No worse-than-start state**: "a cozy game where someone can end up in a worse position than at the start" is not relaxing (5.4).
9. **Content warnings / skip options** for tonal shifts and phobia triggers (Grounded spider option, 4.5).
10. Optional high-stakes content is fine if it is opt-in and appears late (5.2).

## Where Bycer is nuanced
- "A game must have a win and a loss state" is raised as the old definition (2.2) and then set aside: cozy games thrive on the absence of failure; loss is only "going back to a save".
- Low-stakes does not mean no challenge: Stardew has speedruns and 100% as optional hardcore layers (3.4, 5.4).
- Sandbox modes remove stress systems altogether (see [[cozydive-bycer-personalization-and-sandbox]]).

## Stance and applicability
(a) Single-player premium cozy sims, narrative and farm/crafting games; "stakes" mean life loss, progress loss, timers. (b) Mix: Stardew penalty details and Celeste/Artifex Mundi figures are documented; the rules themselves are Bycer's design opinion. (c) Conflicts: [[concept-pacing-and-content-control]] treats energy/timers as deliberate session control and content brake, while Bycer's only endorsed "energy" is Stardew's soft daily one with a morning reset and no purchase; he rejects countdown pressure and permanent loss. [[concept-reward-design-and-psychology]] lists loss aversion as a lever; Bycer says cozy should not use it. [[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]] (grinding, pay to skip) agrees with him in spirit. Artifex Mundi's skip-after-timer is a free analogue of "pay to skip": same mechanic, no payment, which is a useful design contrast. (d) Fit: medium. Our match-3 has lose-a-life/continue moments; the actionable ideas are bounded setbacks, a generous skip/assist after repeated failure (compare DDA in our levels), no countdown on deco choices, free rearranging/undoing of decorations, and never putting meta progress (rooms, cats) at risk.
