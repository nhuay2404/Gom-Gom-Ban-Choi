# User research, motivations, personas and journey maps

Source: [[uxui-brewer-src]], Ch. 5 (pp. 56-61), Ch. 2 (UX researcher, p. 5-6), Ch. 10 (McConnell, Dube, Leah Chalkey).

## Process
- Five design stages: research and planning, concept and ideation, design and production, testing and iteration, launch and post-launch (post-launch widens the test pool and keeps UX work going, pp. 56-57).
- Research recipe (p. 57): align focus with project values; ask "what" for features, "how" for processes, "why" for motivations, one idea per question; pilot-test the questions; ground in data.
- Competitive analysis: appraise similar games for best practice, trends and audience expectations (Jakob's law); in practice screenshot/video each competitor and note pros/cons (Chalkey, pp. 166-167).
- Motivational models: list motivations (action, achievement, completion, immersion, creativity, mastery, fantasy, story, power, social) and decide which to combine; these define personas (p. 58).
- Player experience goals: one-sentence outcomes in the motivation vocabulary, flexible but measurable (the "real superhero" example, pp. 58-59).
- Tools: user interviews, empathy map (see/hear/feel/do quadrants), personas (research-based, several including extremes; name, picture, goals, pain points; test and iterate; six-step recipe, pp. 59-60), journey maps (awareness to satisfaction; hard when the game design is unfinished, p. 60).
- Researcher deliverables: personas, user stories, journey maps, usability reports, heuristic evaluations; the role appears mainly in large studios, mobile and live-service teams; small teams push research to devs/publisher/production, so early decisions rest on assumptions and personal preference (pp. 5-6).
- Survey/affect caveats: affect heuristic means testers' mood biases results, so check wellbeing before sessions (p. 43); survey bias if only hardcore players are sampled (p. 39).

## Reusable takeaways
- Pair a motivation list with a one-line experience goal for each hub (Deco = creativity + completion + fantasy).
- Persona + journey map for the first 7 days of a casual cat player would expose where Home/Map/Deco/Shop compete for attention.

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Large-studio UX research practice from a narrative AAA team; assumes a dedicated researcher or UX designer exists.
- (b) Textbook UX method (design-thinking toolkit); no data, no sample sizes, no method for quantitative telemetry beyond "treat data as a driver".
- (c) Complements [[prodtoolbox-chandler-ux-process]] and [[prodtoolbox-chandler-prototyping]]; adds persona/journey tooling missing from [[mobiledesign-scolastici-design-process-and-mobile-constraints]]. Light on analytics compared with [[concept-analytics-and-kpis]] and [[concept-ab-testing-monetization]].
- (d) Fit: medium. Useful as a lightweight checklist for a designer-led team without a researcher; heavy persona artefacts are overkill for a small team, and casual-audience segmentation is better served by [[cozydive-bycer-ui-ux-and-approachability]] and telemetry sources.
