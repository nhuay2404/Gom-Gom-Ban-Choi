# Bycer, Game Design Deep Dive: Free-to-Play (2023)

Source key: `f2pdive-bycer`. Joshua Bycer (Game-Wisdom critic), CRC Press, 12 chapters, ~180 pp; written end of 2021. Text read in full except front/back matter.

**Nature of the book**: a critic's history + design survey + ethics manifesto, not a data-driven economy text. Examples are mostly hero-collector gacha, MMOG/PC live service and PvP mobile; casual puzzle appears only briefly (Candy Crush, Angry Birds, Flappy Bird, ch. 5). Facts (release dates, revenue claims, laws) are documented; design judgements ("this is unethical", "ads are outdated") are opinion. Each topic page ends with a stance section separating the two.

## Distinctive claims (digest)
- "Fun pain" (deliberately degrading the free game) is bad design and outdated; modern F2P improves the game for payers instead of worsening it for free players (4.5).
- Do not monetize the core gameplay loop; have one primary monetization system; never add new monetization systems post-launch (6.2, 4.6, 11.2).
- Every F2P game needs three phases (onboarding, daily loop, late game), all ready on day one; retention beats first-month revenue (5.5).
- Ethical test "Player-Friendly Design" (PFD): player not forced to play, not pressured to spend, no pay-exclusive content, free players can compete (9.4). Also: every purchase needs a spending cap or a fixed-price alternative to randomness.
- Ads are outdated when competing with 2nd/3rd-gen games (6.1), yet cosmetics sold only for cash is itself questionable (6.4, 9.3).
- Daily loop should take about 30 min, at most 1 hour (9.4.1).
- Casual players are fans of a game, not of a genre: the market is "oases in a desert" (5.3).
