# Source: Vasiuk and Dubrovskyi, Running a Successful Live Service Game (2025)

Book key `liveops-vasiuk`. CRC Press, 2025, ~225 pp. Subtitle "Live Outside of Game Updates". Authors are ex-Wargaming (World of Tanks / Warships / Blitz) LiveOps leaders; the "LiveOps" discipline there was founded in 2017. Text extracted by pdftotext; page numbers below are the book's printed pages. Front/back matter (copyright, acknowledgments, index) skipped.

**Thesis.** LiveOps = frequent, data-driven events and personal offers delivered **without the dev team shipping a client update**, aimed at maximising player LTV (p. 14). Revenue split rule of thumb 40/35/25 between base game, game updates and live services (p. 24). The book is organised around the "3 LAPs of Operations" (live around the Player / the Proposition / the Promotion, ch. 5-6) plus operations management, toolset and analytics, ending with a forward-looking "People's LiveOps" (creator economy, game as platform).

## How to read this book
It is a **PC/console midcore multiplayer** operations handbook (p. 157 says Part IV "focuses primarily on PC games"). Examples are World of Tanks, Fortnite, FFXIV, Destiny, Call of Duty, League. Mobile appears mainly as a platform-fee and push-notification footnote and a "casual games have many DAU, small checks" aside (p. 17). Most claims are practitioner opinion with a few measured anecdotes (59 of 78 monthly WoT campaigns failed, p. 193). For a mobile casual match-3 + deco game, treat it as a **menu of operational ideas to downscale**, not as a design prescription.
