# Stardew Valley case study (Bycer)

Source: [[cozydive-bycer-src]] ch. 3.4 (pp. 26-33), plus 5.4 references.

## Documented design features (as he describes them)
- **Slice of life**: few locations, day-by-day, goals optional; small reachable goals instead of one long arc.
- **Interlinked skill paths** (farming, fishing, combat, mining, foraging): each feeds the others (money buys supplies, ore from combat builds machines, meals refill energy, tools upgrade at the blacksmith). Weak games give separate disconnected quotas ("100 apples, 50 rats").
- **Always something to do, and doing it is rewarded** with something that adds a feature, not just more of the same work. Anti-example he writes: a copper bed costs more and takes longer than a wooden bed with an identical process.
- **Tiers change the process**: crops, then jam/wine, then livestock, each with its own mechanic and bigger payoff.
- **Multiple acquisition routes**: any resource can be bought with money instead of gathered, so money stays a sink late game and players are not forced to repeat early tasks; a twice-weekly merchant sells rare items so a player can skip systems they dislike (combat optional).
- **Removing annoyance as reward**: sprinklers fix hand-watering, with two bigger tiers later.
- **Community Center vs Joja Mart**: two routes to the same gate (Ginger Island), many ways to fill requirements.
- **A moving world**: shop sales, closed days, birthdays, festivals every few weeks, seasons that change crops, year-2 price rises matched to player income (price inflation tied to the player's earning level).
- **Soft failure** (see [[cozydive-bycer-low-stakes-design]]).
- **Characters and writing**: NPC relationships as the main window into the world; romance and friendship cutscenes.
- **Post-launch**: free updates (1.6.15; multiplayer, mastery, new farms), mods; endgame layers (perfection, 100%, gold clock at 10M gold, 500k gold for +1% completion).
- **Multi-level audience**: relaxed play and speedrun categories coexist; "just one more day" flow.
- Caveat he notes: even Stardew lost at least 30% of Steam owners early (3.6, Fig. 3.21).

## Bycer's judgments (opinion)
- Success came from refining Harvest Moon, not copying it; clones that merely copy do badly.
- Optional hardcore layers sit at the very end and are voluntary.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Premium single-player PC/console farming sim; the day structure is play-time based, not real-time. (b) Feature list is documented from his playthroughs; why-it-worked claims are opinion. (c) Overlaps with [[concept-progression-systems]] (horizontal progression, tiers) and [[concept-economy-building-blocks]] (money sinks, converters); his "buy instead of gather" and "year-2 prices rise with income" are compatible with the sinks/inflation discussion in [[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]]. Conflict: Stardew's daily energy cap is a soft reset, not a purchasable timer, unlike energy in [[concept-pacing-and-content-control]]. (d) Fit: medium-high for deco meta design: interlinked zones (garden, kitchen, kinder) feeding each other, each new zone adding a new mechanic rather than reskinning, a gate with two routes, community-center-like collection goals, and mid-game "remove the annoyance" unlocks. Low fit for the day-cycle and combat parts.
