# Budget, burn rate and forecasts

Source: [[prodtoolbox-chandler-src]], Ch8 (pp. 121-128); Ch1.3.7; Ch4.7.

## Forecast and budget (Ch8.2)
Publishers forecast from sales of prior titles or comps (genre, audience, quality), in a spreadsheet covering 12-18 months with variable price/units to test scenarios; goal is the best balance of cost and profit. When building a budget add **flexibility** for estimate variance, involve the team, update often, and keep stakeholders informed so asking for more money is not a surprise. Budget, schedule and resources depend on each other (Ch8.3).

## Cost breakdown (Ch8.3)
Categories: people (usually the biggest), hardware (computers, devices, dev kits, servers), software (engine, DCC, source control, bug tracking), licensing fees (IP, middleware), outsourcing, overhead (rent, insurance, benefits), travel (conferences), business support (lawyer, CPA), plus marketing and publishing (separate budget if a publisher pays). Personnel sheet: number x monthly rate x months by role (Fig. 8.1; example 36 people over 24 months ~ $3.3M people plus other costs ~$0.8M; rates illustrative only). Do not assume cheapest is best: seniors are costlier up front but faster when the time frame is short; juniors make sense with long timelines (training).

## Burn rate (Ch8.3)
Monthly spend; rule of thumb **~$10,000 per person per month** all-in (salary, hardware, overhead), so 6 people ~ $60k/month. Check against forecast; if margin is thin, revisit; early access can bridge funding.

## Tracking and overages (Ch8.4-8.5)
Bookkeeping weekly/monthly; one production person tracks, expense requests go through them; spending authority by seniority; know accounting processes for urgent licences. For overages: do not panic, reallocate (e.g. travel to computers), reduce people on a feature or outsource, communicate early. P&L statements and **breakeven** after launch.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Studio of 6 to 40+ people with fixed monthly rates, publisher or investor funding, and premium sales forecast (units x price). US dollar figures from circa 2018.
(b) Practitioner rules of thumb and an illustrative spreadsheet; no real accounting data (explicitly "rates are for example only").
(c) Conflicts/qualifies: F2P economics are LTV vs CPI/ROAS and payback windows ([[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]], [[concept-analytics-and-kpis]]), not units x price; the forecast spreadsheet technique parallels [[concept-spreadsheet-modeling]] but for revenue is superseded by cohort models. $10k/person/month is US-centric and misleading for a Vietnam-based team. Chandler's burn-rate framing is compatible with lean-startup runway thinking.
(d) Fit: low-medium. Burn rate, runway and the "cheapest is not best" point apply, but a designer-led team inside a company rarely owns the budget. Useful for estimating content cost per deco item/level when planning live ops. Designer-only: low.
