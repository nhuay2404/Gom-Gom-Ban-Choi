# The casual market and its limits

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], 3.2, 5.1-5.3, 4.3, 4.4, 7.1.

## Facts cited
- Casual = easy to play, low stakes, aimed at people other companies ignored; PopCap, Big Fish, The Sims, Artifex Mundi hidden-object games (often women-led). ESA stats: 2020 report, more people play on smartphones than console/PC; casual the preferred genre across ages; ~45% of players identify as female (author's cited figures).
- 1st-gen casual hits: Angry Birds (3-star rating, ad-free purchase), Fruit Ninja and Jetpack Joyride (score chasing, shop + ads), Candy Crush (premium currency for aids that make the game easier), Flappy Bird (ads only; pulled by the creator for addictiveness), Cookie Clicker (idle). Simple games were easily cloned.
- Stage-based progression is popular on mobile because it paces difficulty simply (4.3); non-reflex genres suit touch.

## The author's thesis (5.3)
- Genre fans vs game fans: industry assumed indirect competition ("fans of match-3 buy many match-3"), but casual players stick with the first game that hooks them and money/community "weight" locks them in. Result: oases in a desert; Candy Crush "left no room" for other match-3; this was the MMOG-crash mistake repeated. Hence generations 2-3 searched for undone genres.
- Misconception: "casual" players are not lesser; some play hundreds of hours and compete. He uses casual/core/hardcore for skill at a given game ([[f2pdive-bycer-retention-player-tiers-and-content]]).
- Casual players have the lowest chance of regular spending; for new-player discounts they may buy if cheap enough.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Writes about the 2010s casual boom as history; own focus is hardcore/core hero-collector and PvP. (b) Industry stats documented via ESA; the game-fan vs genre-fan claim is a well-argued opinion backed by anecdote (MMOGs, Slay the Spire), not by switching data. (c) Partially at odds with [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]] / [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]], whose UA/LTV model assumes you can buy installs from genre-adjacent players; reinforces Czerkawski's emphasis on retention ([[concept-analytics-and-kpis]]). (d) Fit: high as strategic warning for a match-3 entrant: do not count on Candy Crush-style genre loyalty; differentiate (cats + deco metagame, like Puzzle & Dragons / Marvel Puzzle Quest added a collection layer) and treat the first 2 weeks as the weight-building window.
