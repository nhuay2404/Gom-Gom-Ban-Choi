# Surprise mechanics: loot boxes, gacha, random progression

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], ch. 8.1-8.4, 9.2, 9.4.4.

## Definitions (8.1-8.3)
- "Surprise mechanics" is the industry's euphemism. ARPG/roguelike randomness differs: the player knows the condition (kill boss, open chest) and the loot is earned through play; in F2P the randomness fires only after spending money or premium currency, like a slot machine. Procedural generation creates an item at reward time; a random drop picks from a fixed list.
- **Loot box**: known contents/odds, pay, reveal; fixed rates (sometimes <1% for top item); drop-rate disclosure now legally required in places. Booster packs differ by guaranteeing rarities. **Milling**: duplicates convert to a currency that buys wanted items, at a cost of hundreds of openings. Reveal animations and rarity cues, and broadcasting others' good drops, add psychology.
- **Gacha**: banner with one featured reward, boosted chance (~50%+ once a top-rarity result occurs); limited banners; rising-rate counters and **pity** (guaranteed after ~100 pulls); ten-pull standardised (better rates/cost for max rolls punishes small buyers); priced higher than loot boxes because gacha rewards are gameplay content. Complete gacha banned in Japan 2012.

## Random progression (8.4)
- Progression is not a consistent curve: lucky players trivialise content, unlucky ones stall and quit; designers do not know player power at any time. Remedies: fixed-utility systems beside randomness (skill choices, upgrade paths), small free gacha currency, upgrade systems independent of pulls. Diablo 3 auction-house-tilted loot was rebalanced and the auction house removed.

## Psychology and ethics (9.2, 9.4.4)
Dopamine anticipation, near-miss/fake-out animations, special offers right after a rare pull (Contest of Champions), investment/sunk cost (about two weeks of play), beginner's purchases limited to 2 days, and spending stories of $1,000+ on one banner. Author: studies link loot boxes to gambling behaviour. PFD demands every random purchase has a cap: either guaranteed handout or fixed price; nothing paid should be available only through randomness.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Hero-collector gacha, loot-box PvP and ARPG; critiques whale-driven design. (b) Mechanics and laws documented; the "addictive" claims lean on cited studies but ethics verdicts are opinion. (c) Largely agrees with [[concept-battle-pass-vip-gacha]] (GD Fresher: gacha pity and disclosure) and Czerkawski ([[concept-reward-design-and-psychology]]: random vs deterministic rewards), but those sources present gacha as a commercial tool with disclosure as the guardrail, whereas Bycer asks for hard spending caps and judges gacha "the most manipulative". Zagal: [[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]]. (d) Fit: low-medium. Our game should not sell randomized paid rewards; transferable ideas: free fixed-utility paths beside any random reward, visible odds, pity/cap if random chests appear in match-3 (e.g. random booster rewards) and avoiding time-limited FOMO offers right after a lucky reveal.
