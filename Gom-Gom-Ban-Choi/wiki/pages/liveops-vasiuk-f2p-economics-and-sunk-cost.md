# F2P economics, sunk cost, resources and the economist/monetizator split

Source: [[liveops-vasiuk-src]], ch. 9, pp. 93-104.

- **F2P defined** (p. 94): accessibility ($0), engagement (most never go beyond free), monetization (a subset spends more than a price tag would have raised). "5% rule" is a design assumption, not a law; a small share of whales gives most revenue; niche games can succeed on dedicated payers. Chapter-17 claim: ~1% of players often produce 70%+ of revenue (p. 220).
- **Insight / Monetization / Optimization** (pp. 95-97): optimisation is the bread and butter of F2P but has costs; **local optimisation** (make monetization highly visible) can harm **global** health (Star Wars Battlefront II 2017 loot box backlash). Optimisation enhances developed features; it must not replace development.
- **Price elasticity** (pp. 97-99): worked example elasticity -2.5; most digital goods near unit elastic; items with zero utility/cosmetic appeal perfectly inelastic at low quantities; **Veblen goods**: rare high-priced items demand rises with price (status).
- **Price discrimination** (pp. 99-100): reserve price and consumer surplus; digital goods have near-zero marginal cost but the studio still bears new-content cost, so classical theory is only partly applicable.
- **Sunk cost** (pp. 100-101): once real money is converted into virtual currency, players spend it with a different mindset; hard-currency surplus can be used to sell content at a higher price-equivalent; new powerful content triggers loss aversion; but constant economy instability erodes value of prior investments. Ethics note: respect investments, new content should enhance rather than undermine them.
- **Two roles** (pp. 101-102): **Game Economist** (designs the system, sinks/taps, inflation control) vs **Game Monetizator / LiveOps** (optimises revenue inside it, dynamic pricing, targeted promotions where a currency is in surplus).
- **Resources** (pp. 102-104): energy/lives: a delay before refill and a cap on stored lives "stimulate players to regularly return"; soft vs hard currency (hard buys premium/skips, converts to soft); taps and sinks must balance: source >> sink makes the game monotone and kills hard-currency purchases; sink >> source feels grindy; monitor hoarding, uncontrolled growth, shortages; no item should be useless at any stage.

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Midcore F2P (WoT premium account, gold, free XP) with an occasional casual farming/match example for energy.
- (b) Textbook microeconomics applied loosely; anecdotes only (Battlefront II). The 1% / 70% figure is quoted without source.
- (c) Overlaps [[concept-economy-building-blocks]] and [[concept-pacing-and-content-control]] (energy). **Differences**: Czerkawski treats soft-currency inflation as a tolerated problem solved with sinks; Vasiuk explicitly splits economist vs monetizator and uses LiveOps events to drain surpluses. On energy, he is neutral-positive (return-driver) with no mention of paid refill, so he does not support or refute hard-currency energy refills; compare dark-pattern critique in [[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]] (appointment/grinding). His use of sunk cost to sell content for hard currency is the kind of lever the Zagal paper flags ([[src-zagal-dark-patterns-in-game-design]]) and he adds an ethics caveat.
- (d) **Medium-high.** Tap/sink balance check, global vs local optimisation, and economist-vs-monetizator separation map to our `economy.mjs` ownership. Sunk-cost exploitation of gems should be used lightly; loss-aversion framing must stay transparent.
