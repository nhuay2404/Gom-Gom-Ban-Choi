# Four keys to fun (Lazzaro) and theories of fun

Source: [[mobiledesign-scolastici-src]], Ch. 11 (pp. 287-290), Ch. 10 (p. 232), Ch. 2 (p. 42).

## Four keys (XEODesign, Nicole Lazzaro)
| Key | What it is | Emotions | Example games named |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard fun | meaningful challenge, strategy, puzzles, goals | frustration, Fiero (triumph) | Civilization, Tetris, Collapse |
| Easy fun | curiosity, ambiguity, incompleteness, detail, rhythm | wonder, awe, mystery | Myst, Tetris, Collapse |
| Altered states (internal experience) | change in internal state via perception/thought/behavior | excitement, relief, clearing the mind, avoiding boredom | Collapse, Crosswords, Tetris |
| The people factor | competition, cooperation, performance, spectacle | amusement, schadenfreude, naches | EverQuest, Mario Kart, Halo |

- Players cite: playing to see how good I am, clearing my mind by clearing a level, feeling better about myself, figuring things out, "it is the people that are addictive".
- Different players come for different keys; games supporting several keys offer a wider range of emotions (Mario Kart).
- The chapter points to Koster (*A Theory of Fun*: play is learning; "games are rules, not graphics") and Caillois (*Man, Play and Games*), and to Meier's "series of interesting choices" (Ch. 10).

## Reusable takeaways
- Map each wiki feature to a key: match-3 clears = hard fun plus altered states ("clear my mind by clearing a level"), deco/cats = easy fun and collection, events/leaderboards = people factor.
- Use the keys as a playtest question set per feature.

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Assumes a general player-experience theory (2003-era research with console/PC games), applied to mobile as a starting point; not specific to F2P.
- (b) Theory based on author interviews with players (qualitative); fine as framing, not predictive; the book only summarises it.
- (c) Complements [[concept-reward-design-and-psychology]] and Bartle types in [[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]]: those give types (killer/achiever) while Lazzaro gives emotions; they do not conflict. Contrasts mildly with [[src-zagal-dark-patterns-in-game-design]], which warns that engagement mechanics can be exploitative; the book's "addictive experience" remark (Ch. 2 p. 42) is more permissive.
- (d) Fit: medium. Useful for feature framing of a casual match-3 plus deco game ("clear my mind" is its core emotion), with the social key currently the weakest.
