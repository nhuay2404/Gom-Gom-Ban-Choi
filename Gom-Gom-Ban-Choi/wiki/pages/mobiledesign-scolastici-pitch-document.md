# Pitch document and the Lilypads sample

Source: [[mobiledesign-scolastici-src]], Ch. 12 (pp. 290-322), Ch. 2 (pp. 40-41).

## Structure (three sections)
1. **Concept** (what, where from): high concept in 3-5 words (e.g., "Jump, eat, and get out of the pond!"); 2-3 references (change just enough of a known mechanic to feel fresh, references may be non-games); pen-and-paper prototype immediately; genre; target audience (never "everyone"); key features list; platform and competitors (find a niche) (pp. 295-298).
2. **Game mechanics** (how it plays): readable without extra explanation, with images; only distinctive mechanics (details belong in the detailed design doc); describe 1-2 unlocks in detail and 2-3 in outline; controls and interface; scoring, achievements, IAP integration; a worked gameplay example with sample levels; screen-flow diagram; game-flow diagram of a match (pp. 298-300).
3. **Tech** (cost and schedule): resolution, asset counts, screenshot mockup, software licenses, schedule and budget (7 people at USD 25/h, 3 months, about USD 42,000 for Lilypads), team resume (pp. 300-301, 318-321).

## Lilypads sample (pp. 302-321)
Peg-solitaire-like tap puzzle for iPhone 4, Unity 4, 2D top-down; target kids 4-13 and women over 30 playing together; time-based par scoring that starts at the first tap (thinking time is free); stars from insects -> virtual currency -> extra levels/skins/redo; freemium (banner, fewer levels, one undo) vs Gold (no ads, extra content, unlimited undo); social score sharing; 64 px sprites; the screen has a lower strip for banners.

## Why pitch
- It forces the designer to move from "nice idea" to a development plan (p. 294). Designing sample levels early exposes mechanic problems.

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Assumes seeking an investor/publisher for a small premium or freemium iPhone game; the template is generic and still usable internally. Dated as of 2026: banner layout, Kickstarter/publisher framing, social-share monetization, USD budget numbers, iPhone 4 specs; no mention of KPI targets, soft-launch plan or LTV, which modern pitches require.
- (b) A fictional example; costs are explicitly "very rough"; no data.
- (c) Differs from [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]] and [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]], where the pitch of an F2P game must show LTV/CPI payback and retention targets; the sample monetization (banner, stars-to-currency, remove-ads) is simplistic compared with [[concept-monetization-strategies]].
- (d) Fit: low to medium. The 3-section skeleton and "high concept in 3-5 words" are handy for feature briefs; the sample game and numbers are not.
