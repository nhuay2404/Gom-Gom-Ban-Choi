# Fun pain, pay-or-wait, energy and ads

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], 3.3, 4.5, 5.4, 6.1, 6.4, 9.1.

## Fun pain (4.5)
- Definition: deliberately leaving or adding pain points (inventory caps, missing QoL, slow progress) so the player pays to fix them. Born when retail games were converted to F2P (Hellgate: London, DC Universe Online tiers, Age of Empires Online locked rewards). Typical form: "free" vs "premium" version of the same game.
- Verdict (opinion): bad design and part of P2W; consumers pushed back in the late 2010s; with hundreds of similar games, a worse free experience just loses players. Modern practice: make a good experience better for payers ("honey, not vinegar").

## Pay or wait (3.3, 5.1)
- Everything takes real time; premium currency speeds it (Clash of Clans: gold/elixir, build timers, gems). Browser origin: 24 h timers, energy that regenerates slowly, buy energy to continue.
- Energy (5.4): capped storage, slow regeneration; out of energy = buy or stop. Doubles as a "you're done for today" signal that stops players burning through thin content. Documented dark use: energy engineered to run out at an emotionally charged moment (Harry Potter: Hogwarts Mystery screenshot, 9.1).
- Intentional difficulty spikes (hard content just before a major unlock, 9.1/9.4.2) are called unethical; you can detect a spike when content before and after is much easier.

## Ads (6.1, 5.1, 6.4)
- Mechanics: ad network SDK, paid per view/click; incentivised ads (watch for resource/continue) preferred to forced; ad-free purchase is a popular IAP (Angry Birds; Flappy Bird earned ~$50k/day from ads).
- Verdict (opinion): ads are outdated; if competing with 2nd/3rd-gen games do not put ads in. Third generation: "required ads" are over (10.1). Misleading ad creatives (Evony, Homescapes-style) are criticised.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Criticises 1st-generation browser/mobile pay-or-wait and ad-funded casual, plus PC MMOG conversions; assumes a competitive market in which hardcore/core players expect generosity. (b) Facts: named games and mechanics documented; "outdated" claims about ads and energy are opinion with no data (no eCPM, retention or revenue split given). (c) Conflicts with [[concept-iaa-ads-design]] and [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]] (rewarded video and interstitials as a core casual revenue stream; ad capping, remove-ads), and with Czerkawski on pinch points and energy as a legitimate pacing tool ([[concept-pacing-and-content-control]], [[concept-monetization-strategies]]); Bycer's "hide-the-wall difficulty spike" critique aligns with [[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]] (pay to skip, playing by appointment). (d) Fit: medium. For casual match-3, lives/energy and rewarded ads are the norm that Bycer undervalues; take from him the discipline: no spike-before-unlock, no energy out at an emotional beat, keep the free experience unharmed, and offer ad-free as an IAP.
