# Narrative theory of quests: Campbell, Frye, Auden, Propp

Source: [[quests-howard-src]], Introduction pp. 6-10; Ch. 3 pp. 67-69; Ch. 5 pp. 104-107.

## The four classic theorists (theory, not design data)
- **Campbell** (monomyth: separation, initiation, return; *Hero with a Thousand Faces*, 1949): popular with designers (Glassner, Rollings/Adams, Novak, Dunniway). Howard distinguishes Campbell's late "quest" (Grail: every knight enters the forest where there is no path, individual and different) from the always-identical hero's journey. Applying the monomyth mechanically makes every game look like Star Wars/LOTR (pp. 6-8).
- **Frye** (*Anatomy of Criticism*): the complete quest romance has three stages: perilous journey with minor adventures; crucial struggle (often a battle); exaltation of the hero. Characters in romance are idealised heroes vs caricatured villains, like chess pieces (pp. 8, 103).
- **Auden** ("The Quest Hero"): quest narratives endure because they symbolically describe our experience of life as a sequence of major/minor goals with uncertain outcome (p. 9).
- **Propp** (*Morphology of the Folktale*, 1928): tales are sequences of *functions* performed by recurring *dramatis personae* (villain, donor, helper, princess and her father, dispatcher, hero, false hero). The quest begins with a *lack* that the hero fills by seeking an object (pp. 9, 68).

## How games turn structure into activity
- Campbell's unified monomyth becomes the **main quest**; Frye's "minor adventures" and Campbell's "road of trials" are stretched into the bulk of gameplay (side quests), repeating separation-initiation-return many times (pp. 9-10, 105).
- Vogler's three-act simplification is "rigid"; quest systems enrich Campbell instead (p. 10).
- *Fable* is the cautionary example: protagonist named "Hero", mentor named "Mentor", villain a Darth Vader composite; archetypes alone give grandeur but weak characters (p. 67). Its writers first wrote a short story on the Campbell schema, then broke it into quests (p. 107).
- Propp is more useful than Campbell/Vogler for game NPCs because it defines characters by what they *do* (functions), which maps onto scripting (p. 68).

## Stance and applicability
(a) RPG/adventure and literary narrative; mythic hero's-journey framing. (b) Literary theory and critical history; no measured data. (c) No direct conflict with other wiki sources, but it is orthogonal to [[concept-progression-systems]] (Czerkawski), where progression is a power/content curve and not a story arc; Howard's "initiation" is the narrative reading of the same level-up curve (see [[quests-howard-meaning-initiation-and-spectrum]]). (d) Fit for casual match-3 + deco: **low**. A cat-themed deco game could borrow the Proppian "lack -> object" idea (a bare room that needs a bed) as framing for milestone quests, but the hero's-journey structure itself is irrelevant to Daily tasks and milestone goals.
