# Acquisition, media mix, partnerships and virality

Source: [[liveops-vasiuk-src]], ch. 7, pp. 55-83.

- **Streams**: paid UA, earned UA (store featuring, influencers, press), owned UA (own channels, word of mouth).
- **Platforms (pp. 56-57)**: ~30% fee; platforms own the audience and may restrict own shops, discounts, price discrimination. Store dynamics: ratings and install/engagement algorithms are winner-takes-all; eventfulness and updates drive featuring; big games monopolise attention in seasonal peaks so time events competitively.
- **Business models (pp. 57-61)**: freemium (accessibility and scale; "free" alone no longer differentiates; light-download-then-stream-the-rest as friction reduction; study funnel). Subscription catalogues (Game Pass, Apple Arcade) give capped LTV, suited to late lifecycle games or monetization-poor genres ("cozy games" named, p. 59). Premium (Van Westendorp price meter, price elasticity example: a 20% discount giving 30% more installs, p. 62; demos; price-down-to-free transitions such as Destiny 2 and Sims 4).
- **Media mix (pp. 62-68)**: brand vs performance table; brand media **irrelevant for hypercasual** (p. 62); performance marketing needs attribution; CPA rises and LTV falls as the market saturates; payback period varies (mobile quick, content-rich games years). Attribution after IDFA relies on fingerprinting with large error. **Triangulation**: attribution + incrementality + survey together; table of 3-way interpretations (e.g. attribution without incrementality suggests cannibalising organic; switch off in a geo to test). MMM is macro and backward-looking.
- **IP and partnerships (pp. 68-69)**: partnerships should not alienate the audience, give mutual benefit, synergise with other levers, grow LTV. Fortnite scale (75+ collabs in one chapter).
- **Influencers (pp. 69-79)**: canvas (goal, success criteria, sourcing, integration type, brief). Do not script the creator; short brief; invite codes and trackable links; flat fee dominates (<10% performance deals); YouTube evergreen vs Twitch time-limited; first 6 days of CPA per video; avoid over-exposure.
- **Virality (pp. 79-83)**: K-factor (>1 means exponential); limit invitations (scarcity makes each invite meaningful, but more invites lowers conversion); FFXIV recruit-a-friend; "a game is intrinsically social and viral, or it is not"; superficial invite mechanics give temporary growth. **What kills virality**: pay-to-win, invasive popups/upsell/ads, bugs, paywalls on key content, toxic community.

## Stance and applicability
- (a) PC/console multiplayer; influencer/Twitch/YouTube heavy; measurement via attribution and fingerprinting rather than mobile MMP/SKAN. Explicitly says brand media is irrelevant for hypercasual.
- (b) Mixed: Apex/Fortnite/WoT anecdotes, frameworks without conversion data; the triangulation table is reasoned, not measured.
- (c) Complements [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]] (CPI vs LTV, payback). Contradicts mobile casual practice on invite spam: GD Fresher deck material on referral hooks should be read with its "limit and make scarce" caveat ([[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]]). Ties to ethics in [[concept-hybrid-models-and-ethics]] (aggressive upsell and paywalls reduce virality).
- (d) **Low-medium.** Our UA is mobile paid + organic; influencer canvas and K-factor are only partly relevant (a cosy cat-deco game suits creators and screenshot sharing though). Useful: "popups, aggressive upsell and key-content paywalls hurt word of mouth", store-rating dynamics, and triangulating UA effectiveness (incrementality over raw attribution).
