# IAP: packs, gift ratio, starter pack, purchase psychology

Source: [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]], stage 2 (slides 12–21), slide 16/27/28 for gift ratio.

## IAP basics
Real money for digital goods. **Hard currency** (bought, or dripped in play) vs **soft currency** (earned). Three store types: **consumable** (gems, lives, energy), **non-consumable** (remove ads, unlock skin), **subscription** (VIP, monthly card). Most IAP revenue is consumable currency. Models: basic packs (5–6), offers/bundles, Battle Pass, VIP/subscription, gacha.

## The 6-pack (slides 15–16)
Example ladder $0.99 / 4.99 / 9.99 / 19.99 / 49.99 / 99.99. Only three packs carry real roles (varies by product):
- **Lowest ($0.99–2.99)**: first-conversion point, sells many units but little revenue. If it is the #1 earner the structure is off (especially high-ARPPU genres). When a time-limited offer or starter pack does conversion, this pack becomes a **decoy** that makes the offer look better and lifts existing payers.
- **Middle ($9.99–19.99)**: reconversion, good value, bought repeatedly.
- **Highest ($79.99–99.99)**: whales, highest gift ratio, speeds payback, and **locks players in** (an unspent balance makes leaving feel like a loss).
- ~6 packs: few enough to compare, enough for meaningful choice.

## Gift ratio (slide 16)
% extra currency vs the cheapest pack (cheapest = 0%); the bigger the pack the cheaper each unit, e.g. +0/10/25/45/70/110%. Compare against other games to see whether one targets whales or minnows; the *step* between packs shows intent. Show "best value / +X% / most popular" because players are rational but won't compute. Compare Czerkawski's table: [[concept-store-and-pricing]] (SKU ladder 0/20/25/40/50%).

## Effective value vs gift ratio (slide 27, Tex Pine)
Gift ratio is paper value; **effective value** is what the resource is worth to the player *now* (power gained or time saved). It decays with progress because upgrade costs and difficulty grow faster than resource income (inflation by design, not oversupply): the same 1000 gems goes 100% → 60 → 35 → 15% from level 5 to 50 in the deck's example. So pack contents must **scale with level/segment** or the pack dies late-game. Items decay even faster unless a fusion system recycles them ([[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]]).

## Purchase psychology (slide 20)
- Buyers are the most engaged, rational players; UX tricks (anchor, decoy, red, countdown) are small nudges, not the driver. "Time-rich vs money-rich" players is a myth: engaged players still buy time-skips. Don't build separate content branches for them.
- **First** purchase is the hardest; after it, repurchase and larger packs get much easier (funnel: install → engaged → 1st purchase → repeat/upgrade).

## Starter pack (slide 21)
Single job: turn a non-payer into a payer, not earn money (judge by first-purchase conversion and LTV of buyers vs non-buyers, never by its own revenue).
- Lowest price tier ($0.99–4.99), **one per account**, with a countdown; effective value set ~**2×** the normal level for newcomers; the barrier is the stigma of paying for virtual goods, so price must be low *and* the value obvious (struck-through price or %VALUE).
- Trigger after FTUE or the first progression wall, not in the first minute. Contents usable right now, not ten levels later.
