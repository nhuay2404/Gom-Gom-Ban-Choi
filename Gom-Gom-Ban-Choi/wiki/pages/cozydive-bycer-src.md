# Source: Bycer, *Game Design Deep Dive: Cozy Games* (CRC Press, 2026/27)

Book key `cozydive-bycer`. Short (about 100 pages, ~42k words), the author's own words: the shortest book of the Deep Dive series, because "cozy" is a thematic genre, not a mechanical one. Bycer is a design critic/analyst (Game-Wisdom.com), explicitly a hardcore-action player who came to cozy as an outsider. Method: case studies of mostly indie PC/console games (Stardew Valley above all), a few quotes from Matthew Taylor (Wholesome Direct curator), and opinion. There is no data of his own beyond Steam achievement-rate observations.

## Thesis in five lines
1. Cozy is a vibe/aesthetic layered on any mechanic; low stakes (no long-term loss) is its defining mechanical consequence (ch. 2, 5.5).
2. UI/UX and approachability decide whether cozy games survive the first 10 minutes; most churn happens there (ch. 4).
3. Progression in cozy = scaling and new subsystems, not escalating difficulty; every tier should add a new mechanic, not "more of the same" (5.4).
4. Players should feel progress "by miles or millimeters" and never end worse off than they began (5.4).
5. Personalization, sandbox/creative mode and open-ended goals widen the audience (5.3).

## What the book is NOT
Not about mobile F2P economy. F2P is deferred to Bycer's separate *F2P* Deep Dive (preface, 3.1) and only touched as the origin of "pain points to motivate someone to spend money" (5.5) and as the source of daily-task conditioning (3.5). No match-3 coverage (Bejeweled is only named as PopCap history). Treat everything here as premium-indie design theory plus critique, and see each page's stance section for how it maps to our game.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Author assumes premium/indie PC, console and Switch games sold once (Steam achievements are his only metrics); mobile F2P is treated as a different book and a source of bad habits. (b) The source page itself is a map; claims on the topic pages are tagged documented vs opinion. (c) Qualifies the F2P sources ([[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]], [[src-czerkawski-game-economy-design]]): it supplies a player-experience counterweight, not an economy model. (d) Fit for a mobile casual match-3 + deco game: medium. Strong on onboarding, personalization, relaxing progression framing; silent on monetization, energy, LiveOps.
