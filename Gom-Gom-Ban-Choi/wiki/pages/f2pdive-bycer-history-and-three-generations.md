# History of F2P and the three mobile generations

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], ch. 2-3, 5, 7, 10. Mostly documented history; the periodisation is the author's framing.

## Roots (ch. 2-3)
- Live service began with MMOGs (EverQuest 1999, WoW 2004, ~12M subscribers at peak). Team Fortress 2 (2007) proved non-MMOG live service: sidegrade items (2008), cosmetics (2009), store (2010), F2P (2011). Item drops: a check every 25 min of play, max 10 h/week; the store lets payers buy exactly the item they want. League of Legends (2009): free from day one, weekly free champion rotation, champions buyable with free or premium currency, skins premium-only.
- MMOG boom/crash: every WoW rival assumed a 10M-player genre market, but WoW's players were fans of the game, not the genre. Subscription + high operating cost pushed survivors to F2P (DDO, LOTRO), which forced redesign of gameplay and monetization.
- Browser/Facebook games (FarmVille 2009, Evony, Kingdom of Loathing, Cow Clicker) brought real-time timers ("5 hours means 5 hours"), energy ("pay or wait"), social invite bonuses and premium "gems". FarmVille's reputation made traditional gamers dismiss mobile for years.

## Generations (author's model)
| Gen | Years | Defining traits | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2007-~2012 | Casual gold rush, simple loops, ads/energy/pay-or-wait, clones | Angry Birds, Fruit Ninja, Candy Crush (2011), Clash of Clans (2012), Puzzle & Dragons (match-3 + gacha), Flappy Bird |
| 2 | ~2012-2016 | Big IPs, PvP, deeper systems, trying to win back core gamers | Marvel Contest of Champions, Marvel Puzzle Quest, Dragon Ball Legends, Star Wars Galaxy of Heroes |
| 3 | ~2016+ | Own a niche/genre; base game good, monetize premium content (rare characters, high-production cosmetics); generous to free players; no forced ads | Azur Lane, Arknights, Genshin Impact, Honkai, Fire Emblem Heroes, Animal Crossing Pocket Camp |

Gen-3 free-player generosity: a free top-rarity character at start, a starter banner with up to 20-100 pulls where you keep one, a ten-pull roughly every ten days. Gen 3 aims at "very few direct competitors" (10.1).

Cited figures (author's claims, not verified here): Angry Birds first game ~$140k dev cost; Clash of Clans ~$400M IAP; Puzzle & Dragons >$7B over the decade; Genshin recouped its dev cost in two weeks, ~$1B first year; Honor of Kings >$10B lifetime. Nintendo's paid Super Mario Run underperformed at $10 (10.2).

## Stance and applicability
(a) Assumes/criticises: PC and mobile live service, MMOG-descended, hero-collector gacha and PvP mobile; casual match-3 is a 1st-generation footnote. (b) Evidence: dates/products documented; generation boundaries and "outdated" verdicts are opinion. (c) Compare [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]] (UA/LTV economics, ads and IAP as normal tools) and [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]]: Bycer calls ads and fun pain obsolete against gen-2/3 competition, whereas GD Fresher and Czerkawski treat rewarded ads as a standard casual tool ([[concept-iaa-ads-design]], [[concept-monetization-strategies]]). (d) Fit for casual match-3 + deco: medium. Useful for market context (Candy Crush as the standard that left no room for rivals; Puzzle & Dragons as match-3 + collection hybrid; Animal Crossing Pocket Camp as a deco-adjacent F2P), little direct design guidance.
