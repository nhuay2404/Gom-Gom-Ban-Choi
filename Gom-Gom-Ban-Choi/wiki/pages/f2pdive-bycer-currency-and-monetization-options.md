# Currency design and the menu of monetization options

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], ch. 6.2-6.4.

## Design principles (6.2)
- Monetization is built on microtransactions; the best are "endless" (the player can never have enough: gacha currency, energy, soft money). Guaranteed items sell at several times the price because once owned they stop being worth anything; the more a player owns, the less they spend.
- Have at least one primary monetization system the player must pay real money for; every extra system raises the "being taken advantage of" feeling. Gacha games: primary = more pulls, secondary = premium skins.
- Do not monetize the core gameplay loop (paid ammo in World of Tanks = P2W). Monetization is all driven by a custom game currency.

## Currency (6.3)
- Free currency (earned, universal) vs premium currency (mostly bought; third-gen games drip small amounts free for progress). Both can split into system-specific currencies (xp, upgrade, gear) and several non-transferable premium currencies (gacha vs cosmetic; double gacha currency for limited banners).
- Critics say multiple currencies obscure real value; one currency is simpler and fairer (opinion). Conversion is nearly always one-way (EVE, Second Life are exceptions needing legal approval).

## Options (6.4)
1. **More resources**: energy refills, soft currency, monthly pass (daily resources; subscriptions returned in gen 3 as daily-resource passes, not access), bundles, limited-time/seasonal sales; permanent unlocks only for premium currency.
2. **Progression methods**: upgrade materials, gacha currency, gear. In gacha, characters cannot be directly bought; duplicates unlock bonuses.
3. **Services**: battle pass ([[f2pdive-bycer-battle-pass-and-seasons]]); benefits end when the subscription ends.
4. **Cosmetics**: potentially the most lucrative; no balancing, bounded only by art capacity; price scales with how much changes (some >$30; skins that alter sound/FX). Limited-time cosmetics that vanish raise value; trading creates secondary markets (TF2 Mac EarPods >$100 for a useless item) and black-market gambling. Author: cosmetics purchasable only with cash is unethical even if non-gameplay ([[f2pdive-bycer-ethics-player-friendly-design]]).
5. **First purchase** is the key event (starter offers; "weight", sunk cost). **VIP** based on lifetime spend is a manipulative casino-comp mechanic (opinion).
- Best outcome: players pay to support a game they love; few examples achieve it.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Hero-collector/gacha and PvP mobile plus PC live service; cosmetics-led monetization for fan-loved characters. (b) Observations about what sells are experience-based, no conversion numbers; the ethical rulings are opinion. (c) Largely consistent with Czerkawski ([[concept-monetization-strategies]]: performance/preference/personalization, hard vs soft currency) and GD Fresher ([[concept-iap-packs-and-starter-pack]], [[concept-battle-pass-vip-gacha]]), but they present VIP and starter offers as tools while Bycer calls lifetime-spend VIP and expiring beginner packs manipulative; Czerkawski accepts hard-currency obscuring with a caveat, Bycer opposes multiple premium currencies. (d) Fit: high. "Endless" sinks for soft currency, deco/cosmetic personalization as the main product (no balance cost for a single-player deco game), first-purchase design and one primary monetization system all map to our match-3 + deco model. Skip gacha-specific advice; consider giving cosmetics a free path to respect his fairness test.
