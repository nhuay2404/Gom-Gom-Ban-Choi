# A relaxing progression curve (Bycer)

Source: [[cozydive-bycer-src]] ch. 5.4 (pp. 83-94), 3.4, 5.2.

## Core idea
High-stakes games progress by escalating challenge and power; cozy games progress by **collection, growth, scale and story**, with no rising difficulty. Taylor: many wholesome games centre on collection, "a kind of escalation" without physical challenge (5.4).

## Rules and patterns
- **Chapters/tasks**: complete X tasks in any order, then advance and unlock new ones; unlimited retries, rarely a loss state.
- **Scaling as progression** (idle-derived): cost and yield grow together; first purchase automates a boring click. Tier ladders (cup of water to golden lobster) give a sense of scale.
- **Tier relevance**: lower tiers can stay in the chain (relevant but no reward for moving up) or be dropped (Nova Lands removes lower-tier requirements after research, freeing space).
- **Each tier a new subsystem**: if 20 trees = 20 statues mechanically, the reward for work is more work. Cookie Clicker: farms and stock market as tier subsystems.
- **Open-ended goals**: state a need ("X") with many ways to fulfil it. "Earn 50,000 gold" is open; "create 3,000 rugs" is fixed. Forager's two intertwined goals (money, XP) mean nothing is unproductive.
- **Progress gates** can exist if multiple routes reach them (Ginger Island).
- **Goal-based tutorials**: early goals like "gather five vegetables" teach by reward rather than formal tutorial; also tell the designer where the player stands.
- **Always something new**: PowerWash Simulator criticised for several final missions with nothing left to unlock. Plants vs Zombies always rewards a level. "Whether by miles or millimeters" feel progress.
- **Fix annoyances early**: do not leave players in the worst version for long; the first upgrade often removes a pain (autoclick, sprinkler). Show before/after scale.
- **Grind** = repetitive work with the reward only at the end; reduce it by allowing multiple tasks toward one goal or by making all activity count toward the goal (Stardew mastery XP counts everything).
- **Idle prestige/reset**: valid only for idle design, not for games with a story or final goal; short-form idle (3-5 hours to ending) is a counter-trend.
- **Endings**: define the end; "final test" in cozy may be just another level or the last story beat; optional post-game for fans; player-defined goals still need a designed start, middle, end.
- **Length is not quality**; padding never works (Taylor, 5.4).
- **Post-release support** (5.4): supplemental content (cosmetics, ways to earn) vs new content (areas, quests); only worthwhile if sales justify; fine to stop.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Premium cozy/indie, automation and idle games; no live service economy; "progression" means unlocks the player can eventually all reach. (b) Patterns are drawn from named games (documented) with Bycer's interpretations (opinion). (c) Aligns with [[concept-progression-systems]] on horizontal progression and the user journey curve; qualifies [[concept-pacing-and-content-control]] and [[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]] where those treat content treadmill and energy as brakes: Bycer treats "pad the length" and long grinds as failures, and treats shortage of unlocks as the main risk, not burn-through. His "never reward work with more work" supports [[concept-reward-design-and-psychology]] guidance on meaningful rewards. His idle-scale endorsement sits near but not equal to F2P timers. (d) Fit: high for the deco meta (zone unlock every ~10 levels each with a new mechanic, no dead stretches between unlocks, a visible before/after) and for level-clearing rewards (PvZ-style "always something"); medium for scaling/idle ideas; low for prestige resets (conflicts with a 100-level story map).
