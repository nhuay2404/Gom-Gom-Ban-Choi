# Source: Scolastici and Nolte, *Mobile Game Design Essentials* (Packt, Nov 2013)

Book key: `mobiledesign-scolastici`. Text extracted from the PDF; chapter-level references (Ch. N, printed page numbers in brackets) are used on the topic pages.

**Thesis.** A practical starter manual for a small indie team making a first mobile game in 2013: pick a platform and engine, split roles, make 2D simple-mechanic games playable in about 3 minutes, prototype early, tune with a few rules, choose a business model, and write a pitch. Authors: Claudio Scolastici (psychologist, ex-tester, indie FPS designer) and David Nolte (23 years in console/handheld, Tetris variants, Faceball 2000 producer).

**Reading note.** The book is generalist and mostly opinion, with a small amount of 2012-2013 market data (Newzoo, Flurry, Juniper, Strategy Analytics) in Ch. 11. About 40 percent of it (Ch. 1, 3, 4, 5, 8 and the three Unity tutorial parts in Ch. 8-10) is tool/OS/language material for 2013 (Eclipse, XNA, Java ME, BlackBerry, Cocos2D/Corona/Shiva, Space Invaders in UnityScript) and is dated; it is only summarised.

## Distinctive claims (digest)
- Satisfying session in 3 minutes or less; save points about every 2.5 minutes; mobile depth is shallow (Ch. 11, pp. 279-280).
- Audio is secondary; every sound must be mutable without harming play (Ch. 2 p. 61, Ch. 11 p. 278).
- Start prototyping from the most frequent player action; lock the design at 3-5 actions, never above 7 (Ch. 2 p. 43, Ch. 9 p. 201).
- Cut a feature on its third failed fix (Ch. 11 p. 274).
- Tuning: one value at a time, extreme values first, code separated from data, a master "global difficulty" multiplier (Ch. 10, pp. 233-235).
- Business: freemium dominant, premium "worse in sales", ads can hurt retention, under 5 percent pay, CPI about 1.50 USD (Ch. 11, pp. 283-286).
- Do not put monetization ahead of the game, but design the game around the business model from the start (p. 285).
