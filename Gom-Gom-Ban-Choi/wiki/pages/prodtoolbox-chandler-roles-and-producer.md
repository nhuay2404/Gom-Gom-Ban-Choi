# Production roles, the producer, developer vs publisher

Source: [[prodtoolbox-chandler-src]], Ch2 (pp. 25-41), Ch11.2-11.4, Preface.

## Developer disciplines (Ch2.2)
- **Art**: art director, lead artist, concept, world builder (sometimes counted as design), 2D/texture, 3D modeler, animator, UI artist, technical artist, marketing artist. Small teams use generalists; large ones specialize. Art-heavy live games (Fortnite skins) need artists to outnumber others up to ~2:1 so several assets are in flight at once.
- **Design**: creative director, lead, level, narrative, writer, systems designer. Designers "receive feedback from all corners" (UX lab, QA, publishing, management) and must filter it.
- **Engineering**: technical director, lead, network, graphics, AI, UI, tools, build engineers.
- **Audio**: audio engineer, sound designer, composer. **UX**: designer, researcher. **QA**: lead, testers.
- Leads (Art/Design/Eng/QA) all "shape scope and requirements" with the producer.

## The producer (Ch2.3)
- Core job: pull the parts together, keep the team on track, remove obstacles, be the information hub. Schedule and budget are tools, not the job.
- Not a content creator; feedback on design/tech/art is lower priority than on scope/schedule/budget, where the producer's opinion carries weight. In disputes: gather input, synthesize, recommend, vet feasibility with leads, communicate the decision.
- **Hybrid roles bias decisions** (a designer-producer finds it hard to cut a feature; engineer-designer conflicts over tech vs feature) - Ch2.3, Ch5.1, Ch11.4. Named as a risk, not forbidden.
- Skills (Ch2.3.1): leadership, communication, organization, "serving others". Training: conferences, management classes, public speaking.
- Ladder (Ch2.3.2): Production Assistant -> Associate Producer (1-3 yrs, daily stand-ups, risk, pipelines) -> Producer (3-8 yrs, looks 2-3 sprints ahead) -> Senior Producer (8-12) -> Executive Producer (franchise roadmap/process).
- Types (Ch2.3.3): generalist, technical, art, creative, publishing, **LiveOps producer** (24/7 game health, reading analytics to plan content/events).
- Publisher-side producer sidebar (Ben Smith): the hardest job is representing both publisher and developer without "going native".

## Publisher functions (Ch2.4)
Financing (milestone payments, recoup then royalties), distribution, marketing/PR, production support (localization, QA), product management (feature roadmap vs sales forecasts, comps), LiveOps (follow-the-sun), community management, customer support. Publisher fee "30% or higher" is justified if a good publisher widens the audience. Publishers judge ROI via comps, state of the game, and developer reputation (Ch2.5.2). Milestone acceptance often triggers payment and can lead to pulled funding (Ch2.5.3).

## Team structure (Ch11)
Org chart with producer over discipline leads; defined roles reduce conflict. Role-clarification exercise (James Lewis): each person answers four questions (how company sees my role, how I see it, resources I need, what I need to know about others' roles), 10-20 min each, publish results (Ch11.2). See [[prodtoolbox-chandler-team-hiring-and-organization]].

## Stance and applicability
(a) Assumes a studio with a separately staffed producer role, discipline leads and (often) a publisher with its own producer; AAA/mid-size console-PC. The ladder reflects EA/Epic-style career tracks.
(b) Practitioner experience; role lists are descriptive taxonomy, ladders are the author's/industry norms ("companies may have different names").
(c) Qualifies other sources: a small designer-led mobile team often has no producer; the hybrid-bias warning matters most there. Agile schools (Scrum) fold the producer into Product Owner/Scrum Master; Chandler keeps the producer as separate owner of scope/schedule/budget. Product management here (Ch2.4.5) overlaps what [[concept-analytics-and-kpis]] and [[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]] assign to live-ops/economy designers.
(d) Fit: medium for a small mobile casual match-3 + deco team. Useful vocabulary (LiveOps producer, product manager) and the "designer wearing a production hat" bias warning; but the long ladder, publisher-producer dynamics and 20+ role titles are irrelevant. For a designer-only role: medium - know what a producer needs from you (scope estimates, cut lists), but you are not expected to do production.
