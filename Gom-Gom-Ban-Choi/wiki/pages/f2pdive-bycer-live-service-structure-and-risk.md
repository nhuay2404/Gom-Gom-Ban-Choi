# F2P structure, live-service risk, and the future

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], ch. 4 (4.1-4.4, 4.6-4.7), 11.6, 12.

## Structure (4.2)
- F2P = no initial purchase, revenue from microtransactions. Every F2P game is live service, not vice versa. Account level gates content and tracks phase. No designed end: instead a "daily loop" of daily/weekly tasks ([[f2pdive-bycer-mobile-methodology-and-three-phases]]).
- Core gameplay is simple, 5-10 min bursts (mobile 1-5 min, ch. 5); monetization usually sits in the systems around the loop, not in the play itself.
- Genres that scale: multiplayer, RPG/abstracted progression (infinite number growth), CCG, tower defense, hero collector, idle, stage-based casual (easy pacing control). Fixed linear content "twisted" into F2P shows the seams: Shadow of War's final-chapter difficulty spike pushed spending and the monetization was removed after backlash (4.4). Mobile rejects $10+ upfront prices.
- Scalability reasoning: new maps/characters give more value than a new 2-hour quest.

## Live-service rules (4.6)
- Keep producing content while profitable; usually no sequel (it splits the fan base; carrying paid content over is costly or impossible; unfair loss = never spend again).
- Decide the monetization model early; build progression and balance around it.
- Never introduce a brand-new paid system after launch, especially one promised absent (Payday 2 loot boxes had to be reverted). Adding content to existing systems is fine.
- The game changes over months/years; bad updates drive mass quitting.

## Risk/reward (4.7, 11.6)
- Success breeds success; a bad launch rarely recovers (Evolve closed 2016; FF14 the exception). Multiplayer needs population on day one. Competitors are judged against the leader's latest version; retention "weight" (money, community) protects incumbents (Slay the Spire, WoW).
- End of life: maintenance mode, or shutdown with months of notice, monetization off, best characters given. Players have long memories; purchases vanish at shutdown. A good ending preserves goodwill for the next title.

## Future (ch. 12)
- Possible regulation (US 2020 bill, Japan complete-gacha ban 2012, China drop-rate disclosure 2016) could end mobile F2P if aimed at minors. Console/PC ports: stricter store rules, hacking/security, UI redesign. Play-to-earn/NFTs: author expects the first wave to fail; static NFTs add no utility; only dynamic player-minted designs (Project Eluene, his friend's game) look plausible. He admits the section may date quickly.

## Stance and applicability
(a) PC and mobile multiplayer live service, MMOG logic; assumes teams able to support years of content. (b) History and cases documented; "never add systems post-launch" and NFT forecasts are opinion. (c) Compare [[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]] (Czerkawski: GaaS content planning, sinks), which treats live ops as economy management rather than a risk narrative; [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]] runs A/B-tested offer changes after launch, which sits uneasily with Bycer's "no new monetization systems later" (reconcile: new offers inside existing systems are fine, a new paid system is the issue). (d) Fit: medium. Day-one content depth and "design monetization early" matter for a deco/match-3 game that will live-op for years; population and multiplayer risk largely do not apply (single-player).
