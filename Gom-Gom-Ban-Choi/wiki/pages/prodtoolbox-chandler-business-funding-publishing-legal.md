# Business context: financing, revenue models, platforms, pitch, legal

Source: [[prodtoolbox-chandler-src]], Ch1 (pp. 3-23), Ch3 (pp. 43-53), Ch9 (pp. 129-143).

## Development framework (Ch1.2)
Four phases: create the prototype, establish requirements, make the game, launch. Phases overlap; think iterative cycles. With an MVP approach there is always one feature set in planning, one in execution, one in launch (needs a strong pipeline).

## Financing (Ch1.3)
Self-funding, credit cards (risky), bank loans (hands-off, need business plan; SBA), crowdfunding (Kickstarter/Indiegogo/Fig; backers are owed regular updates; refund risk), investors (friends/family, angels, VC, accelerators vs incubators), grants, early access (not a main funding source; needs a playable game), publishers (own IP, heavy involvement). Sidebar (Della Rocca): investors fund *opportunities* - three kinds: noncommercial, commercial (fixed product, linear revenue curve) and company (needs exponential potential: online, endless, community). Publishers judge your ability to deliver on time/budget.

## Revenue models and platforms (Ch1.4-1.6)
One-time payment, subscription, DLC/episodic, **free-to-play**. F2P: 2-3% of players pay; needs strong loops meshed with microtransactions; "the most impact on gameplay mechanics" of all models; stigma of whales/pay-to-win; works with AAA quality. Mobile: short sessions, simplified UI, store distribution via Apple/Google; typical 70/30 split. Platform holders require certification (TRCs); resubmission "happens quite frequently"; approvals 2-20 days (Ch4.4). Revenue model must be chosen at concept (Ch5.3.7).

## Publisher relationship (Ch2.5, Ch4.4)
Milestone payments, deal memo -> contract, vetting visit (references, process, technology, team) (Ch9.6). Developers should regularly send builds/status or risk funding withdrawn. If a late change is requested, scope its cost and ask for more money.

## Pitching (Ch9)
Goals: full funding (rare), publishing only, partial funding. Know what you will not compromise (IP, core features). RFP/bid process for work-for-hire. Pitch materials: elevator pitch (hook, who you are, high concept, 2-3 USPs, team), one-page executive summary, 15-minute deck (10 slides), full deck, supporting docs. **A playable demo "trumps any documentation"**; hands-on only if stable; vertical slice is what publishers want to touch (Ben Smith sidebar). Market research, competitive analysis, production plan (budget, schedule, risks) condensed. Cold outreach: 4-sentence email, follow up twice.

## Legal (Ch3)
- Game mechanics ideas (e.g. Match 3) are not protected; expression (art, code, audio, story) is. Genre clones are not IP violations unless assets are copied (Ch3.2).
- Copyright (register to enforce), trademarks (generic -> fanciful strength), patents (20 yrs), trade secrets and NDAs (unilateral vs mutual; publishers often refuse to sign).
- Contracts: development contract (financial terms, milestones, IP transfer, QA/localization responsibility), work-for-hire vs licensing (Jay Powell checklist), employment, EULA ("as is"), TOS (cheating, gambling, real-money trading, bans).
- Licensors: need brand bible, approval time in schedule, limit revision count, auto-approval after X days (Taylor sidebar, Ch4.4).

## Stance and applicability
(a) Assumes an independent or publisher-dependent developer trying to raise money and release premium or F2P games on Steam/console/mobile stores around 2018-2019; US-law legal chapter.
(b) Practitioner experience plus industry rules of thumb; legal content is explicitly "not a substitute for a lawyer".
(c) Conflicts: F2P treatment is thin and sceptical in tone (stigma, 2-3% payers), where [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]] and [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]] treat F2P as the primary frame with LTV/UA math; neither covers UA cost. Ethics of F2P is only a sentence; see [[concept-hybrid-models-and-ethics]] and [[src-zagal-dark-patterns-in-game-design]]. The "ideas not protected" point is relevant to clone-style design (see wiki memory on cloning the dig game) but is US-based and not legal advice.
(d) Fit: low-medium. Funding, pitching, boxed product and console certification do not apply to a team already funded internally. Worth taking: the MVP/continuous feature-set cycle idea, IP-vs-mechanic distinction, and the principle that a playable build beats documents. Designer-only: low (business/legal belongs to management).
