# Retention, player tiers, content planning and collabs

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], ch. 11 (11.1-11.5), 5.5, 7.4.

## Retention and the three tiers (11.1)
- Key success metric is retention, not profit or peak players. Community is made of casual (new, little spend, may buy cheap new-player offers), core (daily players; buy boosts or good deals), hardcore (late game, mastered; spend selectively on what they lack and on supporting the game; biggest whale potential; main feedback source and streamer base). Casual -> core -> hardcore flow is needed; failing to retain casuals signals a core problem and demands excellent onboarding. A hardcore-only game cannot grow (Evolve). In PvP, core players buffer casuals from hardcore.
- Design focus after launch shifts to hardcore content, but all three must be served.

## Two kinds of content (11.2)
- Designed content (maps, characters, cosmetics, events) vs player-generated (mods, other players, streamers, creative spaces such as Animal Crossing). Cycle: new content -> interest + revenue -> more content.
- Avoid "one and done" content except story chapters that give free players premium currency. Special events are best: seasonal, monthly, side stories, mini-games, anniversaries, returning-player events; should be self-contained, open to all with tiered difficulty, reward free players too. New systems can renew interest but not new paid systems; new progression must be reflected in matchmaking (C&C4 level-gated units). Do not touch onboarding/daily loop once built.

## Long-term support (11.5)
- Reliable cadence (gacha: new character or rerun every 1-2 months; always an event running); avoid dumping months of events at once; ship UX/QOL improvements to all regions simultaneously (against delaying QOL in global versions); major updates as marketing events (TF2 teaser week, Payday annual mega event, Fortnite seasons); self-contained episodic content that stays available. Stopping updates stops growth. Late game should exist at launch.

## Pop culture and collabs (11.4)
- Mature hits attract IP collabs (licence fee/revenue share assumed; brand-safety demands; licences expire: DbD kept Stranger Things perks free after the licence ended). Cosmetic-only collabs avoid balance problems; collab FOMO events are unethical. Plan for expiry; once a customer owns content it should not be removed.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Multiplayer/hero-collector live service with passionate hardcore communities. (b) Tier definitions and cadence are experience-based; no churn or revenue data; claims about collab contracts are explicitly assumptions. (c) Maps onto [[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]] (Czerkawski: GaaS content planning, event cycles, Bartle types), [[concept-analytics-and-kpis]] (retention curves) and [[concept-core-loop-and-metagame]]; Bycer's casual/core/hardcore tiers are skill/commitment based whereas GD Fresher's segments (dolphin/whale, [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]]) are spend-based; he accepts hardcore as the spending target while still warning against whale-only design. (d) Fit: medium-high. Our "hardcore" = late-level/deco completionists; plan content cadence (new levels, deco areas, events) so there is always something running, keep events self-contained and inclusive, cosmetic collab seasons are a natural fit, and protect the onboarding/early loop from later changes.
