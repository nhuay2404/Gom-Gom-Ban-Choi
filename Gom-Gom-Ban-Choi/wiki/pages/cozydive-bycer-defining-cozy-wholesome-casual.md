# Defining cozy, wholesome and casual (Bycer)

Source: [[cozydive-bycer-src]] ch. 2.1, 3.5, 5.1, 5.5, 6.2.

## Definitions (mostly Bycer's own proposals)
- **Casual design** (5.1): easy to start, low interactivity, often waiting (Farmville-style: place things, wait). Not necessarily gentle in tone: casual games about killing zombies exist and are not cozy.
- **Cozy** (5.1, his qualifier): the player has a relaxing time and the game carries no message or theme to take away. Example: PowerWash Simulator, quarter-view idle games.
- **Wholesome** (5.1, quoting Matthew Taylor): hopeful; may include hardship or sadness if there is reason for hope; personal and message-bearing (Coffee Talk, To the Moon, Wanderstop).
- Taylor's view (5.1): no real difference for most players; cozy is a deeply personal feeling tied to associations and context. Bycer concedes there are no agreed metrics (unlike roguelike vs roguelite) and mislabelling is not fatal.
- Cozy began as an offshoot of casual (2.1) and became its own style through the 2010s.

## Thematic, not mechanical (5.5)
- Cozy is thematic like horror: any mechanic can be re-skinned. FPS can be wildlife photography or power washing; deck-battlers can be dance-offs (Ooblets); restaurant sims range from Diner Dash (casual) through Cook, Serve, Delicious (hardcore) to Venba (wholesome).
- Aesthetic is delivered by presentation: friendly town and no monsters (Little Rocket Lab) vs lone survivor (Factorio) on near-identical automation mechanics.
- Rule: pick one experience; you cannot be relaxing, nail-biting, cozy, brutally hard and horror at once (5.5). Changing the UX is building a new game (4.2).

## Tone consistency (3.6, 5.2) - Bycer's opinion with anecdotal support
- Marketing a game as cozy and delivering a surprise horror twist, or a violent plotline in a party game, is "tonal whiplash" that angers the audience. Dark content is acceptable when motivated (Wanderstop panic attacks) and flagged by content warnings or skippable.
- Do not claim moral superiority for wholesome games (3.6); they are not "better".
- Combat can be cozy if optional, low-consequence and thematically fit (Taylor, 5.5; Stardew, Rune Factory).

## Casual stigma and monetization (5.5)
- Bycer attributes the stigma on "casual" to late-2000s browser/mobile games that were easy to start, then introduced difficulty spikes and pain points to motivate spending. His implied contrast: indie cozy games are casual in input but do not use pain points as a revenue lever.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Premium indie PC/console framing; "casual" is discussed as history of Farmville/PopCap and the F2P stigma. (b) Definitions are explicitly opinion/proposal; history anecdotes are documented but selected. (c) Contradicts the usual F2P view that casual+deco is just a retention funnel: Bycer says a game that creates pain points to sell relief is not delivering cozy, which cuts against pinch-point monetization in [[concept-monetization-strategies]] and parts of [[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]] (pay to skip, grinding). Agrees with those sources on disclosure/consent. (d) Fit: medium. Useful for a brand/tone checklist ("cozy" promise vs match-3 fail screens, timers); but our core loop is a puzzle with a fail state, so we are casual+cozy-themed (deco meta, cats), not "no-failure cozy".
