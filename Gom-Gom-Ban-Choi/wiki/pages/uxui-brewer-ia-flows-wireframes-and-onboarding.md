# Information architecture, flows, wireframes, prototypes and onboarding (FTUE)

Source: [[uxui-brewer-src]], Ch. 5 (pp. 63-71), Ch. 6 (front-end menus pp. 86-88), Ch. 2 (UX architect p. 7-8).

## Claims
- Information architecture: structure content to match the player's mental model; clear hierarchy, consistent labels, group similar elements, order by importance (p. 63). Deliverables: hierarchy and navigation map (p. 7-8). Role often absorbed by UX/design director.
- User flow chart: the path to complete a task; shows when to present information and lets players finish in as few steps as possible; also surfaces technical needs (p. 63).
- Menu depth rule: any number of screens is fine if flow is one-directional; if players must navigate backwards, keep menus two or three levels deep (p. 87). Front-end menus are a chance to foreshadow, teach loops (Bioshock lighthouse, Half-Life 2 background tracks progress, Left 4 Dead intro as tutorial). Cinematic animated menu transitions slow navigation (p. 87).
- Interaction design rules (pp. 63-65): consistency and clarity (write an interaction guide for the project), KISS, make things obvious, rely on recognition/affordance, set the tone (neither patronising nor complex), feedback including micro-interactions.
- Wireframes: low-fidelity (no art, fast, test assumptions, collaborate) and high-fidelity (real content, patterns). "If you use just one tool from this chapter, make it a wireframe" (p. 68). Raise fidelity for non-design stakeholders; a director called a grey wireframe dull (p. 69).
- Digital prototypes: nothing beats putting something interactive in hands; reduces wasteful iteration given the cost of production time (p. 69).
- Onboarding/FTUE: first five minutes are critical; FTUE extends into early gameplay and is revisited with every new feature; ask accessibility needs first, subtitles on by default, settings available immediately; stop "tiresome tutorials" (forced move parroting, text walls); add context and narrative (Helldivers 2, Spider-Man opening drop "press to swing"); do not bombard with all options (pp. 69-70).
- Effective UX checklist: predictable functionality, difficulty is not linear, do not overdo tutorials, recognise micro-interactions, narrative feedback more meaningful, do not silo UX decisions, never overwhelm, do not iterate without testing, keep changes small (pp. 70-71).
- Start screen: first second matters; sets tone and DNA (p. 86).

## Reusable takeaways
- Draw the user-flow chart for shop purchase, rewarded-ad claim and Deco placement; count taps.
- Treat each zone unlock (kitchen, kindergarten, etc.) as a mini-FTUE with progressive disclosure rather than a tutorial dump.
- Menus: Home/Map/Deco/Shop as a tab shell fits Brewer's "tab menu reduces layers" (p. 104-105).

## Stance and applicability
- (a) AAA/console practice; FTUE examples are action games; mobile onboarding data (D1/D7 funnel drop-off) is absent.
- (b) Practitioner method plus a few named game examples; no measurements.
- (c) Matches [[mobiledesign-scolastici-ui-design-and-diegesis]] (screen-flow chart before art, grayscale wireframes, back button) and [[prodtoolbox-chandler-prototyping]]; mobile onboarding in [[concept-analytics-and-kpis]] would add funnel metrics. "Don't overdo tutorials" supports [[cozydive-bycer-ui-ux-and-approachability]].
- (d) Fit: medium-high. Flow charts, wireframes-first, progressive disclosure and "narrative tutorial" ideas apply directly to a hub-based casual game; lacks mobile-specific FTUE benchmarks.
