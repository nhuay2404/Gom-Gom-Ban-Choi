# Battle Pass, VIP/monthly card and gacha

Source: [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]], stage 4 (slides 44–52).

## Battle Pass (slides 45–47)
- Popularised by Dota 2's Compendium (2013), exploded with Fortnite. Each season: **Free** and **Premium** tracks; play earns XP to unlock tiers.
- Buying psychology: high perceived value (~$5–10), progression FOMO (locked premium rewards next to free ones), commitment/sunk cost (play to "not waste" it), **endowed progress** (pre-grant the first tiers).
- Design variables: season ~4–8 weeks, ~50–100 tiers (depends on game); XP tied to daily play and missions so it drives engagement; reward curve spread evenly + attractive milestones + best reward at season end; price ~$5–10, plus "pre-unlocked tiers" or "Premium+" bundles; pace not too easy (loses value) nor too hard (buyers who can't finish regret and don't buy next season). The deck's example runs a 14-day pass (tier vs playing-days chart).
- Compare Czerkawski's version (60-day seasons, pass money flowing back via final-tier currency): [[concept-monetization-strategies]], [[case-marvel-snap-and-the-finals]].

## VIP / subscription products (slides 48–49)
Three forms: **Monthly card** (buy once, receive currency daily for 30 days → builds a login habit), **VIP tier** (points from total spend unlock lasting perks), **auto-subscription** (weekly/monthly renewal). Perks: daily currency, +% rewards, no ads, extra slots, cosmetics. Strengths: steady recurring revenue and retention (you must log in to collect). Ethics/law: clear renewal terms, easy cancel, no hidden auto-renew ([[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]]).

## Gacha and pity (slides 50–52)
Random pull by rarity (example rates ~80% Common / 15% Rare / 4% Epic / 1% Legendary). Pull: variable-ratio reward, near-miss, collecting/showing off. Problem: long dry streaks frustrate or cause out-of-control spending. **Pity**: **soft pity** raises the rate gradually during a dry streak; **hard pity** guarantees an item after N pulls (also gives payers a known cost ceiling). **Rate disclosure** is mandatory in many markets and some regions treat loot boxes as gambling. Metrics: average spend to rare item, % of players hitting hard pity (high % = soft pity set too late). See the roll-table maths and coupon-collector estimate in [[concept-spreadsheet-modeling]].
