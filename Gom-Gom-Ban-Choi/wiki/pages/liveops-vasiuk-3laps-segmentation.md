# 3 LAPs framework and player segmentation

Source: [[liveops-vasiuk-src]], ch. 5-6, pp. 41-52; ch. 12 pp. 160-162.

- **Generic path to own path** (p. 41-42): all players share a generic path (shop, missions, regional events); LiveOps adds a personal path. Do not promote every character/item, only those relevant to level and preferences; promote special events to those with matching progression; show expensive items to whales.
- **Three LAPs**: live around the **Player** (segments), the **Proposition** (value per segment), the **Promotion** (communication). Four questions: who is the target audience; what player problems do we solve (engagement, onboarding, winback, monetization); what exactly is offered (product and price, where price can be time or advocacy, e.g. refund promise); what should the offer look like (keep it simple, time it). Segments should be small enough yet stable for at least a week/month (p. 51). Each LAP is mirrored by an ops function: DataOps, EventOps/StoreOps, CommunicationOps (p. 52).
- **Segmentation types** (pp. 42-43): marketing (demographics, psychographics), motivational (Quantic Foundry), behavioural (whale / dolphin / minnow), lifecycle (beginner / mid / endgame). Start with macro segments (new MAU, MAU, lapsed) for statistical power, add micro later.
- **Descriptive vs predictive** (p. 43): reporting uses descriptive segments, but operational use needs **predictive segments** (likely behaviour in 1-4 weeks), e.g. "likely to stop within days because session length is falling".
- **Segment catalogue (pp. 44-45)**: newbies (simplest and hardest; must-have FTUE campaigns, first-purchase packs); lapsed (recent/engaged, casual low-engaged who return for big beats, disinterested = brand marketing); churners (falling games per day, friends leaving); non-payers likely/unlikely to pay (a non-payer still has value: concurrent users, social proof, referrals, content); active payers likely/unlikely; **resource deficit** (offer resource packs) vs **resource surplus** (deplete via promotions/discounts/events). All segmentation decays over time and must be re-tuned.
- **Channels table (pp. 48-50)**: in-game promo best conversion but most abusive if untimed; launcher; email (cheap, low conversion, reaches long-lapsed); social custom audiences; companion apps. **Omnichannel = unified creative + integrated (next message adapts to behaviour)**. Regional relevance means coordinating the generic path with local holidays, not redrawing every asset.

## Stance and applicability
- (a) PC/console midcore multiplayer with launcher, email, whales; assumes a data platform and predictive models (see [[liveops-vasiuk-toolset-and-analytics]]).
- (b) Practitioner framework; no conversion figures. The segment definitions are well reasoned but untested here.
- (c) Overlaps [[concept-price-discrimination]] and [[concept-offers-and-merchandising]] (segment-based offers). More conservative than the GD Fresher deck on whale targeting: it says target the minority but warns against designing everything around the 1% (see [[liveops-vasiuk-peoples-liveops-and-attention]]). Differs from [[concept-iaa-ads-design]]: no channel guidance for ad-supported users.
- (d) **Medium.** Resource deficit/surplus segments and newbie-vs-veteran offer separation are cheap and highly relevant (energy/boosters/coins economy). Email, launcher and whale-VIP programmes are not. Predictive segments need volume; start with rule-based ones.
