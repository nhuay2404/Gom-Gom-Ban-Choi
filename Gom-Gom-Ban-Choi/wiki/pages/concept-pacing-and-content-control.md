# Pacing and content control

Sources: [[src-czerkawski-game-economy-design]] ch. 2 (pp. 39–40), ch. 4 (pp. 103–105), ch. 5 (pp. 119–128).

- Both failure modes exist: content too easy to get (players burn it, nothing to chase) or too slow (never see features). Burning is the easier trap and shows up in spreadsheets and feel; designers get too close to the product and over-give.
- Brakes: progression/account level, hard caps (e.g. The Finals caps held VR currency so players must spend), **energy systems**.
- **Energy system** (pp. 103–105): energy depletes per session, refills over time or for hard currency. Real purpose is *session control and habit*, plus content pacing; direct monetization is secondary and crude. Disguised variants: Clash Royale chests (limited slots, open timers; you can keep playing but lose rewards).
- **Content treadmill**: players' demand for new content keeps rising; cosmetic-only monetization (personalization) is the most treadmill-prone. Mitigations:
  - replayability of existing content: completion mechanics (stars/grades, collectibles), random variability, new mods for old weapons;
  - long-term upgrade systems: random resource drops + multi-stage upgrades (Marvel Contest of Champions). Only works if each upgraded item is a distinct mechanic (works for cards, poorly for rifles);
  - late-game content for experienced players (multiplayer after story, raids);
  - rarity system to multiply assets;
  - energy system to cap consumption.
- **Cycling content**: new = promoted; later in regular store; later discounted; finally rotated out or reused. Digital goods don't wear out, so obsolescence is built in via rarity tiers ([[concept-store-and-pricing]]).
