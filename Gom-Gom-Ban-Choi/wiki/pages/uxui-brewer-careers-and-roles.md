# Career material (education, portfolio, applications, interviews, seniority)

Source: [[uxui-brewer-src]], Ch. 2 (seniority pp. 14-18), Ch. 3 (pp. 19-27), Ch. 8 (pp. 126-137), Ch. 9 (pp. 138-154), Ch. 10 (pp. 155-168), Ch. 11 (pp. 169-174). Summarised briefly on purpose; little game-design content.

## Summary
- Education: degree, independent course, self-directed, internship; none guarantees a job; portfolio and demonstrated skill outweigh diplomas; since 2022 layoffs the junior market is saturated; networking and mentoring (Limit Break, BAFTA YGD) matter.
- Seniority ladder: intern, junior/associate, mid (2-4 years or one cycle), senior, lead (people) vs principal (craft), director. Real seniority = relationship with feedback; avoid entitlement and gossip (pp. 14-18).
- CV: 1-2 pages, tailored, no skill progress bars, PDF. Portfolio: ten rules (best work only, curated path, consistent layout, result first then process, credit collaborators, describe your role and learning, show motion, be prepared, personality). Portfolio quality should match the target studio; no-experience routes: coursework, redesign a game screen, game jams, modding, case studies (pp. 126-135).
- Job search: values first, studio maps, careers pages, no "easy apply", speculative applications, QA as non-trivial entry, recruiters, tracking sheet, red flags ("we are a family", unpaid long art tests) (pp. 138-144, 149-150).
- Interviews: research the studio, practise, ask open "good" questions, salary negotiation tactics, contracts, rejection is not about you, day-one checklist, impostor syndrome (pp. 144-154).
- Expert interviews (pp. 155-168): common themes: simplicity and clarity, accessibility from the start, communication, finish projects, "best UI is invisible" (Robinson, Johnson), UI/UX are entertainment plus usability (Plater), user is not static.
- Closing: communication is the most valuable skill; fail fast; kill perfectionism; be kind (pp. 169-174).

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Aspiring UI/UX hires in UK/US AAA, console/PC studios; salary and visa advice is UK/US specific.
- (b) Practitioner opinion and anecdote; expert quotes are short and unreviewed.
- (c) No overlap with design sources; a minor tie to [[prodtoolbox-chandler-team-hiring-and-organization]] (hiring side) and [[uxui-brewer-workflow-style-guides-and-teams]] (role definitions).
- (d) Fit: low. The user is a game designer, not hiring or seeking a UI/UX job; value limited to the role map and "feedback attitude" ideas for team culture.
