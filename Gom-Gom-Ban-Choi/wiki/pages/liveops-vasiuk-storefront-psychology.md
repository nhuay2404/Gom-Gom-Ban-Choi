# Game storefront: Four Ps and nine psychological levers

Source: [[liveops-vasiuk-src]], ch. 9, pp. 115-122.

- **Four Ps** (pp. 115-117): Product (permanent vs occasional; item library with sale cooldowns), Place (premium shop in client/web/launcher; commodities next to the loop that consumes them), Promotion (simple copy, call to action), Price (supply is virtually infinite so elasticity and competitor prices matter; price adjustments often "disguised" to ease payment, offer insurance, gamify).
- **Store categorisation / choice overload** (pp. 117-118): jam experiment (24 vs 6 options); chunk into 5-9 meaningful categories (Weapons, Premium accounts, Boosters, Special).
- **Decoy effect** (p. 118): a third, pricier bundle makes the mid option look like the bargain; works for high-consideration items, not impulse commodities (Monster Hunter Rise sale example).
- **Free offer** (pp. 118-119): lowers risk and drives store traffic; risk of anchoring the price at zero; progressive daily free claims bring players to the store.
- **Gifting / reciprocity** (p. 119): gift should be unexpected, personal, valuable; **give first, then ask**; do not overuse or it feels manipulative.
- **Anchoring** (p. 120): a $100 pack makes $15 look reasonable; zero-price history raises resistance to later paywalls.
- **Reduce friction** (p. 120): fewer steps, prefilled fields, wallet, align offers with existing habits (bundle the resource players buy before an event).
- **FOMO and loss aversion** (p. 121): limited time or quantity; rentals (own it for a while then pay to keep).
- **Coupons** (pp. 121-122): informational advertising plus price cut; players choose; coupon use patterns reveal preference; use coupons as milestone rewards.
- **Social proof** (p. 122): soften wording ("most adventurers prefer"), personalise to friends.

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Premium-item PC store with a web portal and launcher; high-consideration cosmetic/vehicle purchases, with some commodity packs.
- (b) Behavioural-economics claims cited from classic studies (jam experiment); no own A/B numbers. The tone is "toolkit", with brief ethical caveats.
- (c) Strongly overlaps [[concept-store-and-pricing]] (anchoring, decoy, Weber) and [[concept-offers-and-merchandising]]. **Conflicts/qualifies**: FOMO, loss-aversion rentals and "gift then ask" sit in dark-pattern territory per [[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]] and [[src-zagal-dark-patterns-in-game-design]] (pre-delivered content, appointments); Vasiuk gives only light cautions ("do not overuse"). Choice-overload chunking aligns with the GD Fresher UI hierarchy guidance ([[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]]).
- (d) **Medium-high.** Chunking categories, decoy on bundles, friction reduction (one-tap buy), and gifting-first suit a deco shop; decoy only on high-consideration bundles. Apply FOMO sparingly and with disclosure.
