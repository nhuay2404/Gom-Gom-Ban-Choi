# Hiring, onboarding, picking leads, strike teams, remote work

Source: [[prodtoolbox-chandler-src]], Ch10 (pp. 147-155), Ch11 (pp. 157-165).

## Hiring (Ch10)
People are the main resource; hire for complementary skills and willingness to collaborate; HR handles logistics, producer defines needs and interviews. Job descriptions: real requirements only, inclusive language, diverse teams "are more successful". Sources: recruiters (external for senior), job boards/IGDA, **referrals**, job fairs, interns (assess before offering). Process: resume screening (gaps, short stints, verify credits via references, beware padded claims), phone screen (expectations match; ask for concrete examples of problem solving, conflict, risk evaluation), **discipline tests** (short; reduce unconscious bias; work-for-hire clause; do not demand a full design doc), face-to-face with pre-agreed questions, debrief demanding specifics, follow-up interview if doubts, flexible start date.
Onboarding: buddy, scheduled lunches, introductions, play the game, 30/60/90-day goals; full ramp 2-3 months. Training for leads (people management, conflict) and technical skills. **Retention: trust and respect matter more than money**; recognition, autonomy, flexible hours, home working, proper hardware.

## Roles and leads (Ch11.2-11.3)
Role clarification exercise (see [[prodtoolbox-chandler-roles-and-producer]]). Leads manage content creators rather than create; pick **the best manager, not the most skilled craftsperson** (it is easier to teach technique than people skills); weak leads silence the team; if cannot replace, work around (producer covers, unofficial junior lead). Sidebar (Jennings): best leads have compassion, check on people daily, teach.

## Organization (Ch11.4-11.5)
Small teams: one person in several roles (producer-designer) - expect internal conflict. Producer-lead org chart is clear and grows people but **silos disciplines**. **Strike teams**: cross-discipline groups owning features (UI team, player-character team, enemy team), with all resources inside, agile methodology, a strike-team lead, reconfigured at sprint/milestone end; discipline leads remain responsible for mentoring and career. Seating by feature rather than discipline (Ch12.4.3).

## Remote (Ch11.6)
Pros: wider talent, fewer interruptions. Cons: trust, fragmented communication, time zones, language. Practices: in-person kickoff, multiple channels, virtual water cooler, same-time stand-ups, clearly defined work, same source control and approval workflow, daily check-in, overlapping hours, IT contact. Sidebar (Sowers): regular structured communication is the biggest force multiplier; peer review should have one owner and be fast.

## Stance and applicability
(a) US studio hiring through HR and recruiters; teams from ~10 to hundreds, with discipline leads and later strike teams; pre-2020 remote practice (before pandemic normalization).
(b) Practitioner experience; leadership claims rely on interviews and general management books; retention claim ("money is not the primary motivator") is asserted, not measured (the Game Outcomes Project is the only data source, Ch12).
(c) Qualifies: small mobile teams (often under 10) are cross-functional by default, so strike-team advice is nearly the default; hiring/HR content is irrelevant when the team is fixed. Partly in tension with data-driven live ops where small cross-functional "squads" own metrics, not features. Agile sources (Scrum) support the cross-functional/self-directed team claim.
(d) Fit: low-medium. Take: pick leads by people skills, cross-discipline feature teams with all roles included, one owner for review/approval, remote-work communication habits. Skip: recruiting funnel. Designer-only: medium - designers often become design leads and should know the lead-selection and role-clarification advice.
