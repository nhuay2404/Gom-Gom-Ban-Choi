# Live service, value chain, forecasts and validation (Part I)

Source: [[liveops-vasiuk-src]], ch. 1-3, pp. 9-34.

- **Eras (ch. 1, pp. 9-14)**: game-as-a-club (arcade, late 60s-mid 80s), product (boxes), service (2000s-mid 2010s, success unit shifts from sales to ARPU), platform (mid 2010s on: streamers, UGC, Roblox, UEFN). Live service wins on playtime share (Newzoo: 60% of playtime in games 6+ years old, p. 2).
- **Definition (ch. 2, p. 14)**: LiveOps = frequent data-driven events/campaigns with no dev-team involvement, to maximise LTV. Three scopes: acquisition (new MAU), retention (MAU), monetization (gross revenue). Three forces: LiveOps practice, operations management, toolset. Key capabilities: segmentation, balance management, **remote config**, personalized offers, analytics, A/B tests, events, community.
- **Caution on going live (pp. 2-3)**: not every game should become a service; asking players of a self-contained IP to treat it as a hobby can disappoint; live ops costs budget and culture change.
- **Seven guiding principles (p. 21-22)**: evolve continuously; design systems so LiveOps can operate without devs (store changes, missions, passes) via reusable self-sufficient units; commitment beyond launch; guided by insights (consistency of data beats perfection early); quality perception is subjective; **revenue follows engagement**; forecast creatively, manage costs wisely.
- **Value creation chain (pp. 24-27)**: base game (value capped by launch content, theme-park analogy), game updates (new modes, collaborations, time-limited modes, ideally quarterly/monthly), live services (events, personal offers, storefront, outside the client). Rule of thumb revenue split **40/35/25**. Content in the base game caps value; updates are costly in dev time; live services are cheap and fast.
- **Financial model (pp. 28-31)**: align on what "good" is for investors; reverse-engineer revenue as acquisition x retention x monetization with "goalposts" from platform base, CPI, NPS (NPS 30% means each player brings 0.3 organic players, p. 29), competitor D1/D7/D30; match cost structure to business potential (do not pick the idea then back-fill a forecast, p. 30); platform fee ~30%; revenue from new content should exceed R&D, marketing under (new players x LTV) (p. 31). P&L skeleton p. 31.
- **Validation (pp. 31-34)**: every test without real players is biased; per-metric validation ladder (wishlists, CPI test in soft launch, playtests, alpha D1-3, CBT/soft launch D30, spreadsheet/Machinations economy sims). **Noise vs bias**: use the same imperfect measurement consistently rather than changing method; track incremental improvement over absolute values; early free users are the best players so set higher early targets and expect lower ROI and virality at scale (p. 33).

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Assumes a **PC/console multiplayer live service** (WoT, Fortnite, Call of Duty) with a studio large enough to staff a separate LiveOps function; revenue drivers are skins, premium vehicles, passes.
- (b) Opinion plus rules of thumb (40/35/25, NPS 0.3 viral factor) stated without data source; the noise/bias point and cohort-degradation point are sound statistics practice.
- (c) Consistent with [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]] on LTV vs CPI. Qualifies [[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]] (Czerkawski plans ~6 months ahead) by offering a split of dev-update vs no-update work. Its "build systems so LiveOps never needs devs" principle matches our `remote()` config design (see CLAUDE.md economy rules).
- (d) **Medium.** Principles 2, 4, 6 and the validation ladder transfer directly to a small team; the 40/35/25 split and the P&L bits are generic. Soft launch is the one validation tool it says is mobile-only, which is our case.
