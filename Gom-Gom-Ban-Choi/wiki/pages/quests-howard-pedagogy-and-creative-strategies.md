# Pedagogy, four creative strategies, Clive Barker and transmedia

Source: [[quests-howard-src]], Ch. 6 pp. 139-146; Conclusion pp. 147-150.

## Classroom method (Ch. 6)
Howard's English class adapts Pynchon's *The Crying of Lot 49* into quest-game design documents (six pages mixing game design doc and interpretive essay) plus a prototype in Aurora Toolset, an ARG website and Flash. Lessons: (a) adaptations work best when players *enact* some events in space instead of replaying the text linearly; (b) a labyrinth of "all interpretations" is not a design: students must commit to rules, goals and a thesis; (c) the Aurora plot wizard/journal system, built around quests, produced the most functional prototypes, while Flash gave rough results for lots of work; (d) one student replaced "kill" with an "interpret" combo move that humanises the enemy but often fails (pp. 139-145). Teachers could use quests across literature courses.

## Four strategies (Conclusion, pp. 147-148)
1. Create a dream-like, surreal space that immerses and offers challenge and progression from start to goal.
2. Design symbolic objects that function in gameplay and reveal fragments of the story ("rod of seven parts").
3. Develop characters with crucial functions (give, help, impede), sparse memorable dialogue, and some choice of which tasks to accept and how.
4. Script meaningful interactions where strategic play also conveys ideas (magic system, virtue system).

## Barker and transmedia
Clive Barker: quests are nonlinear narratives, moving from outward to inward knowledge; games can fill gaps of a novel and reopen paths the linear book had to close; transmedia storytelling uses plot holes as spaces for other media; "games are literary" (pp. 148-150). Howard envisions designers working in a transmedia "multiverse".

## Stance and applicability
(a) Humanities teaching and indie/mod RPG creation; audience of literature students and designers using toolsets. (b) Anecdotal classroom results from one instructor; no controlled evidence. (c) No overlap with F2P sources such as [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]] or [[concept-monetization-strategies]]; the book never discusses monetization, retention or short-session design. Counter-point to [[mobiledesign-scolastici-design-process-and-mobile-constraints]]: Howard's workflow is document-first, tool-first and slow, while mobile prototyping is playable-first. (d) Fit for casual match-3 + deco: **low**. The four strategies are for narrative RPGs; the most portable item is strategy 3's "give some choice of which tasks to accept and how" (order-free milestone quests), already covered on [[quests-howard-quest-systems-and-quest-log]]. Nothing for Daily tasks.
