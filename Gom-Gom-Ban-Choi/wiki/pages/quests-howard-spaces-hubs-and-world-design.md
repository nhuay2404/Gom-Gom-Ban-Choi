# Quest spaces: hubs, breadcrumbs, towers, dungeons, dream spaces

Source: [[quests-howard-src]], Ch. 2 "Spaces", pp. 45-65.

## Principles
- Movement toward a goal through space is the common thread of quest definitions (Aarseth). Level design becomes **world design** once games leave dungeons for open geography; plan increasingly remote, exotic locations whose quest-givers are more relevant (p. 45).
- Natkin: "a level is made up of a collection of quests"; a quest is a succession of objectives, obstacles and solutions distributed through a maze-like space (p. 46).
- **Tiered/leveled spaces** (towers, ziggurats) give an instinctive sense of progression ("go up", seek unexplored). *Oblivion* gate towers: same goal each time (take the sigil stone from the top), learnable architecture and naming conventions let players navigate without a map. Spatial levels mirror character levels (pp. 46-48).
- **Quest hubs**: known places (cities, forts) where quest-givers hand out tasks and to which the player returns; the player moves outward to a dangerous place and back for a reward. Starting hubs are small with easy quests matching low level; later hubs are farther, more dangerous, with harder quests (pp. 48-49). *Morrowind* places the final-quest site at map center behind impassable mountains with one gate, signalling endgame difficulty spatially (p. 50).
- **Breadcrumb quests** (Kaplan, WoW): tasks that lead players into new areas, used deliberately to encourage exploration (p. 49).
- **Dungeons/labyrinths**: descent = greater peril and reward; locked gates opened by keys found later. Howard distinguishes *spatial* branching (cheap, fine: events attached to places) from *temporal plot branching* (explodes exponentially; criticised by Glassner/Crawford) (pp. 50-52).
- **Allegorical and dream spaces**: spaces should be fantastic, dreamlike, labyrinthine, allegorically arranged, progression-through-ascent, organised by hubs (p. 50, 54-56). Obstacles of terrain should stand between player and target (p. 60).
- Level-design checklist (p. 58): embedded meaning; balance of obstacles vs exploration vs sense of progression; hubs for distributing tasks. Everything placed should have a purpose; avoid clutter; check unbroken paths (p. 63).
- Practical: tutorials for Aurora/NWN2 tilesets and terrain editors; avoid micromanaging details at the cost of mood (pp. 59-64).

## Stance and applicability
(a) 3D/tile RPG worlds with traversable geography and an avatar. (b) Design commentary and game analysis; no data. (c) Closest relative on our side is [[concept-core-loop-and-metagame]] (hub-and-spoke metagame) and [[concept-pacing-and-content-control]] (gating content with level). Howard's hub escalation (small village to big city) parallels our zone unlock ladder (garden, living room, kitchen...), where distance equals difficulty/cost; breadcrumbs parallel nudging players toward a newly unlocked zone. Contrast with our "no level gate on Daily/Quests" rule: Howard's hubs *do* scale quests to level. (d) Fit for casual match-3 + deco: **low**. The hub/breadcrumb idea is only loosely reusable: a milestone quest can point the player to a newly unlocked deco area ("breadcrumb to the kitchen"), but there is no spatial navigation challenge in our game and Daily tasks need none of this.
