# Playtesting and churn (Bycer)

Source: [[cozydive-bycer-src]] ch. 4.3-4.4 (pp. 50-60), 4.2, 5.2.

## Playtesting practice (opinion from experience)
- Test from rough prototype onward; placeholder art is fine if play is engaging.
- Ask the questions: do they enjoy it, understand it, struggle, like, dislike, specifics. Bring targeted questions and focused builds; record sessions, ideally without watching live; look for stuck points and shared pain spots.
- Trusted friends first, then newcomers. Cycle testers: once played, they cannot give a fresh-player view. Hardcore fans articulate wants but tolerate pain points; do not design for only the loudest 100.
- Feedback is vague ("this game sucks"): infer root causes from patterns. Ignore neither the echo chamber of praise nor lone negatives.
- "It is rarely the consumer's fault" if the basics are not understood after onboarding.
- Steam Early Access is not a playtest: the game is already for sale. Live-service/updated games need QA and tests for every addition.
- Limit: small studios lack reach; use forums, clubs, colleges.

## Churn
- Definition (his): players who leave negatively and cannot be counted on for future games. Measured via Steam achievement rates.
- Pattern (documented via achievements): biggest drop in the first 5-10 minutes then stabilising; examples of 100% to 65% to 63% to 45%... and games losing 80% within 20 minutes. Breakout niche games show a larger early spike (88 / 55 / 53 / 52.1) because curious non-fans try them. Shadow Tactics lost over 60% within 90 minutes; Celeste and Stardew each lost around 30% early (see [[cozydive-bycer-low-stakes-design]]).
- Benchmarks (opinion): for an 8-10 hour game, a 30-35% clear rate is a modest success; quitting at 8 of 10 hours is healthy, at 15 of 80 is not.
- Life, not frustration, also causes quitting; players may feel done after seeing the main points.
- First-game results are not the true audience; budget the next game for realistic repeat rate.
- Indie vs AAA metrics differ (prestige, marketing, tolerance).
- Fix the problem in the game (marketing event, extra spend) or carry the lesson to the next game.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Premium games measured by Steam achievements, one-time purchase, studio sustainability; "churn" is defined by him differently from F2P (no D1/D7/D30 cohorts, no LTV). (b) Documented examples of drop-offs, but small and unsystematic; benchmarks are rule-of-thumb opinion. (c) Conflicts with the definition in [[concept-analytics-and-kpis]] and [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]] (retention curves, ARPDAU); the shape (steep first-minutes drop) agrees, the numbers are not comparable. Echoes [[concept-ab-testing-monetization]] on cohorts and listening to data. (d) Fit: medium. Use the qualitative practices: playtest with fresh players every build, record and review, watch where players get stuck (our level-difficulty bot and DDA metrics are the quantitative twin). Do not import his clear-rate benchmarks.
