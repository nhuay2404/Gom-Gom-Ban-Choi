# Economy building blocks

Source: [[src-czerkawski-game-economy-design]], ch. 1 (pp. 5–14).

**Game economy**: inter-related systems of resources, currencies and goods plus activities for players; more game design than economics. Goals (pp. 6–8): direct engagement, long-term experience, access to and control of content distribution (the "brake"), intrinsic fun, adaptability/growth post-launch, access to monetization.

| Element | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Soft currency | Earned by playing; used for upgrades, gameplay-linked buys | Usually one main soft currency; F2P adds more over time |
| Hard currency | Bought with real money (microtransactions) | Small amounts dropped in play to teach its use; mostly for cosmetics |
| Goods | Tangible owned items (weapons, apparel) | Durable or consumable ([[concept-monetization-strategies]]) |
| Resources | Precursor items collected for a purpose (crafting materials, XP) | XP is a resource, not a currency: it cannot be spent |
| Source (faucet/tap) | Creates currency/resource/good from nothing | Main balancing task is the soft-currency source |
| Sink (drain) | Permanently removes them | Counters inflation; must stay credible in the fiction |
| Converter | Destroys one thing and creates another (crafting) | Source + sink at once, not a kind of sink |
| Trader | Moves existing things between entities (player ↔ player) | Creates/destroys nothing; vendor buying is a sink, not a trade |

Rules of thumb
- Resources/goods have controlled distribution so need fewer blatant sinks; converters work well for them.
- Selling goods back to an NPC for a fraction of the price is a sink for the good.

## Designing the economy (pp. 18–22)
Ask first: target audience, intended emotions (include frustration/disappointment, pride/envy), what players do. Then walk **Activities → Intensity → Rewards → User journey**. Intensity maps effort to reward value; reusing rewards across unlike activities blurs easy vs grind rewards. Sketch the journey on paper: onboarding (contrived, minimal effort), mid-game (less predictable), end-game.
See also [[concept-core-loop-and-metagame]].
