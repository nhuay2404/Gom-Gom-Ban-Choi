# Ethics of F2P and Player-Friendly Design (PFD)

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], ch. 1, 9 (9.1-9.5), 12.3. The book's signature contribution; mostly opinion, anchored on documented examples and laws.

## Thesis
F2P is not inherently evil; mechanics are tools, ethics is in implementation (preface, 1.1, 9.3, 12.3). Games are addictive by nature and that is fine; the line is a game designed to demand time and push spending beyond what the player accepts. Developers deserve profit but not all of a person's money. An ethical F2P will earn less than an exploitative one but build a healthier fan base. Author found few positive examples and says no game passes all four PFD points (not TF2, PoE or Warframe).

## Anti-consumer practices (9.1-9.2)
Gambling-industry hires; slash-and-burn games; intentional difficulty spikes; energy running out at tense moments; free premium currency to condition first spend; spending hidden until the daily-loop or late phase; PvP/guild pressure; monetization-integrated matchmaking (Activision patent); manipulative targeting of children (Smurfs' Village, Hogwarts Mystery); expiring beginner purchases; FOMO; fake-out gacha animations; building investment (play about two weeks).

## The four PFD questions (9.4)
1. **Forced to play?** Daily loop should be ~30 min, max 1 h; weekly or pooled goals; avoid guild and time-slot obligations; do not tie progress to specific hours (time zones).
2. **Pressured to spend?** No spikes designed to punish non-spenders, no arms-race content, never lock items players earned (Age of Empires Online), no monetization-tilted matchmaking, clear purchase contents and visible odds, avoid many currencies.
3. **Pay-exclusive content?** None: no spender-only shops or VIP content; cosmetics should have some free route; free earnable cosmetic currency; avatar body options never paywalled; TF2's mistake: key drops only for payers.
4. **Can free players compete?** New content should not be strictly better; new systems must reach free players; competitive maps not paywalled; free rotations (LoL); every option viable. Also: every purchase needs an intended spend limit, a pity or fixed price, never random-only for paid goods; do not design for whales alone.
Plus cosmetics ethics: defaults designed to look drab and paid skins creating peer stigma among kids (Fortnite bullying reports).

## Regulation (9.5)
Japan complete-gacha ban (2012), China drop-rate disclosure (2016), 2020 US bill, Shadow of War and Battlefront 2 backlash. Industry self-regulates only under threat; warns that a broad law targeting content "aimed at minors" would end mobile F2P overnight.

## Stance and applicability
(a) Criticises gacha/PvP mobile and PC retail-with-microtransactions; protects the free, time-poor and child player; assumes games with cosmetics and long live-service tails. (b) Evidence quality: laws, patents and named incidents documented; the four-point test, 30-60 min ceiling and "cosmetics-only-for-cash is unethical" are opinions without data; he even notes his own favourites fail. (c) Aligns with [[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]] (Zagal: pay to skip, appointments, monetized rivalries, disclosure) and the ethics section of [[concept-hybrid-models-and-ethics]] (GD Fresher: pure-IAP vs hybrid, legal traps); conflicts with Czerkawski and GD Fresher where they present spend-to-skip pinch points, expiring starter packs, VIP tiers and segment price discrimination as standard tools ([[concept-monetization-strategies]], [[concept-iap-packs-and-starter-pack]], [[concept-price-discrimination]]); also stricter than them on cosmetics, currency obscuring and whale targeting. (d) Fit: high as a checklist. For a casual match-3 + deco game aimed at a broad (possibly young, female-heavy) audience, run it as a red-flag review: no spike-before-unlock, deco items with free earnable routes and a visible price, one soft/one hard currency, capped paid sinks, no game-affecting expiry of earned items, gentle dailies. Treat "no pay-exclusive content" as aspirational; limited-time starter offers are a trade-off to decide consciously.
