# UI/UX, onboarding and approachability (Bycer)

Source: [[cozydive-bycer-src]] ch. 1.2, 4.1, 4.2, 4.5 (pp. 41-68).

## Concepts
- **UI** = how the player physically interacts (controls plus GUI); **UX** = the feel/emotion the whole game produces. UX is the throughline with the core loop; changing it is building a new game (4.2).
- **Primary vs secondary actions**: primary verbs get their own input; secondary are context-sensitive on one "use" button.
- **GUI**: show only needed info, frame edges, keep the main view clear, use dynamic elements (e.g. placement preview showing bonuses, GUI that moves away from the action). If players need an external wiki for fundamentals, the UI/UX is wrong.
- **Accessibility vs approachability**: accessibility removes outside barriers (colourblind mode, subtitles, no strobe); approachability makes the game easier to play (quality of life). Every approachability feature is accessible, not vice versa. Approachability has no agreed metrics (opinion).
- **Core-loop test**: anything pulling the player out of the core loop (menuing, repetitive pickup, manual tool switching, inventory sorting) accumulates annoyance that never goes away. Examples he cites: ARPG compare/sort/filter, Diablo 3's rechargeable potion, context-sensitive harvest, hotkeys, auto-marking NPCs on the Elden Ring map (patched in).
- **Aesthetic clash**: a peaceful B&B sim that forces 30 minutes of itemised spreadsheets, or a detailed sim without them, both clash with the declared UX.
- **Platform redesign**: Pikmin and Diablo console UI rebuilds opened new audiences.
- **Stressful-content options** (Grounded spider swap) and warnings.
- Hardcore fans resist approachability changes; the designer decides the line. "It should never be difficult to perform basic actions"; frustration is worse in cozy because relaxation is the promise (4.5).
- Studying method: play many games of the genre, note what annoys and what delights; do not rely on watching footage.
- "A mobile games UI/UX is a great way to see how fast games can onboard players" (5.1).

## Stance and applicability
(a) PC/console games with keyboard, mouse or gamepad; mobile referenced once as an onboarding benchmark. (b) Largely opinion backed by anecdotes from AAA/indie examples; no usability data. (c) Mostly consistent with [[concept-analytics-and-kpis]] (early retention) and [[concept-ab-testing-monetization]] (measure, don't assume); not in conflict with F2P sources. Qualifies "friction as monetization lever" in [[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]]: Bycer treats friction as a defect. (d) Fit: medium-high. Transferable ideas: remove non-core menus (our map to deco to shop flow), context-sensitive actions, dynamic placement previews in Deco, colourblind-safe tile art, no strobe/flash in match effects, a first-session path free of confusion. Touch input specifics (no keyboard/hotkeys) are not covered. Combine with [[cozydive-bycer-playtesting-and-churn]].
