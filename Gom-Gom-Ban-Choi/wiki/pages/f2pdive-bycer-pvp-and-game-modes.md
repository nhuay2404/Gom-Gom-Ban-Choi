# PvP, competition and late-game content

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], 7.1-7.2, 7.4, 9.1, 11.3.

## PvP design (7.2)
- Second generation made PvP the engine of long-term content: unlimited content when you have a population. Introduced mid-game, matters in late game.
- **Asynchronous** (most mobile: attack another player's AI-controlled team) vs **synchronous** (needs infrastructure and population). Async balance problem: AI defenders are weaker, so defenders are favoured with time limits; top players are those who reached the top first with the best defence. Synchronous needs bots as fallback or time-windowed events (bad slots exclude time zones).
- Incentives: currency rewards (free + premium) for high rank; PvP-only currency; guild vs guild; seasons with rank reset, per-rank rewards, end-of-season rewards, esports invitations. Top players can end up not needing to pay: "rich get richer".
- Matchmaking by skill is essential; do not mix casual and hardcore ([[f2pdive-bycer-retention-player-tiers-and-content]]); ELO is the usual basis. Fixed meta kills competition (Gwent weather redesign); constant new content to move the meta.
- Cautions: if the daily loop is only PvP you repel non-competitors; Activision's 2017 patent for monetization-aware matchmaking is cited as a prime unethical example; guild pressure forces time and spend on members.

## Long-term content (7.4)
New maps and characters are cheapest; PvE uses new chapters, limited-time story events and expert-level stat-boosted content, plus raids. Every daily-loop content type needs a late-game version; a neglected system becomes a weak link that nobody uses.

## Stance and applicability
(a) PvP/hero-collector mobile and PC competitive F2P; assumes server populations. (b) Mostly descriptive of documented systems; the Activision patent is a real filing, but ethical readings are opinion. (c) Little overlap with Czerkawski/GD Fresher, which focus on solo economies; Marvel Snap case in [[case-marvel-snap-and-the-finals]] is the nearest wiki analog; Zagal's "monetized rivalries" ([[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]]) matches Bycer's rich-get-richer critique. (d) Fit: low. Our game is single-player; borrow only the idea of seasons/events as live-ops frames and async leaderboards at most (see [[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]]).
