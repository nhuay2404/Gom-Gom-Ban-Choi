# Progression systems

Source: [[src-czerkawski-game-economy-design]], ch. 2 (pp. 30–39).

Purposes: teach players, build a play habit, gate access to content (the brake), plan the player's experience, and tie to analytics (account level = where a player is in their life cycle).

**User journey curve** (Fig 2.3): value/power gained vs time is fast early, levels off, near-flat at end-game, so end-game needs *different* engagement (PvP, raids, seasons with resets and rotating rewards) and unique rewards.
- Onboarding/early: pre-defined, linear, everyone gets roughly the same quantities; rapid visible progress; hook with an intense action sequence.
- Mid: habit reinforced, players form strategies, use social features, plan spending.
- End-game: new modes, leaderboards, seasons; something early players never get.
- F2P shows sharper phase separation; premium can be subtler since the player already paid.

**Content control via account level**: lock cosmetics, areas, features, modes behind levels.

Types
- **Vertical**: direct power increase (upgrades, stronger weapons).
- **Horizontal**: more variety/depth, no strictly better item (CoD weapon mods). Risk: accidental vertical progression.
- **Rarity** (Common → Legendary): cheap way to expand a catalogue (one model × 5 stat/colour variants); signals quality; builds attachment to Epic/Legendary; for cosmetics signals "coolness". Rarity forces artificial obsolescence ([[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]]).
- **Skill trees**: skill points spent on a graph of abilities; limit equipped skills for depth.
- **Perks**: equippable bonuses; can randomise weapons for variety.
