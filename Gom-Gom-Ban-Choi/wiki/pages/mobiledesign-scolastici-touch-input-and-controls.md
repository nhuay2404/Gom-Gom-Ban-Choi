# Touch input and mobile controls

Source: [[mobiledesign-scolastici-src]], Ch. 6 (pp. 127-146), Ch. 7 (pp. 163, 166), Ch. 11 (p. 277-278).

## Claims
- Controls plus graphics most affect gameplay experience; unintuitive or laggy controls kill sales (p. 127).
- Gesture vocabulary: single tap, double tap, long press, scroll, spread/pinch, pan, flick, multifinger tap/scroll, rotate (pp. 130-133). Examples: Fruit Ninja (swipe), Angry Birds (drag-release), Temple Run, Doodle Jump (tilt).
- Sensors: accelerometer (supported by essentially all phones, so a safe mechanic basis), GPS/AR, camera, microphone (blow/voice novelty) (pp. 134-139).
- External controllers/cases: optional extras only, never a requirement (pp. 139-143).
- Virtual buttons eat screen space; prefer gestures and sensors; keep controls simple; make buttons big enough to press (Street Fighter IV's large buttons as example) (Ch. 7 p. 163, Ch. 11 p. 277).
- Evergreen options: calibration and left-handed reconfiguration; rotate support may require a second layout (pp. 166).
- Genre expectations in UI: do not break a genre's learned convention unless necessary (p. 149).

## Reusable takeaways
- Direct manipulation (tap/drag/swipe) is the core idiom; avoid on-screen d-pads for a match-3 or placement game.
- Expose handedness/sensitivity options where relevant.

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Assumes 2013 first-generation capacitive multitouch, accelerometer-as-novelty, controller add-ons (iCade, Sensus), eye tracking and brainwave readers as "future". Dated as of 2026: gamepad support is native OS-level, haptics, 120 Hz and large screens exist; eye/brain input remained niche.
- (b) Descriptive survey and examples; no usability measurements (the only quantified rule is the "30 seconds / 3 questions" test in Ch. 7 p. 149).
- (c) No conflict with other wiki sources; complements [[concept-sinusoidal-motion]]-style feel work and the project's drag-card input (CLAUDE.md `card-drag.js`). Thumb reach and one-handed portrait are not discussed here.
- (d) Fit: low to medium. Gesture list and "avoid virtual buttons" apply; sensors, controllers and future tech do not.
