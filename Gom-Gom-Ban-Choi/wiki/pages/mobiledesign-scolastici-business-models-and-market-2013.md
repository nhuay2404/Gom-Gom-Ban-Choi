# Business models and market (2013)

Source: [[mobiledesign-scolastici-src]], Ch. 11 (pp. 281-286), Ch. 12 (pp. 297, 303, 309-311), Ch. 2 (pp. 50, 55).

## Market facts quoted (2012-2013)
- Newzoo: more than 500M mobile gamers, about 175M paying, market USD 9B in 2012. Juniper: 64.1B game downloads by 2017 vs 21B in 2012. Strategy Analytics: Android 64 percent of Q1 2013 handset sales; iOS leads in Japan and in spend (p. 281-282).
- Flurry: women prefer solitaire, social turn-based and management; men action/strategy/betting; solitaire/endless/social turn-based have high retention; 61 percent of US payers male (p. 282).

## Models (pp. 283-285)
- Premium (about USD 0.99; Angry Birds, Doodle Jump): "predominant" at the start, now underperforms (only Minecraft paid in the top-20 downloads in Feb 2013); use only for licensed/unique content or very short sessions.
- Freemium: dominant (15 of top-20 grossing, e.g., Clash of Clans, Bejeweled Blitz); IAP of consumables (time skips, currency, mana); needs value over time or virality; gather stats on users, session time, spend and adjust prices and targeted offers.
- Ad-supported: banners, interstitial video 15-30 s (careful: hurts retention), offer walls (free content for actions); works for essential utilities and long sessions; Temple Run cited.
- Hybrid: freemium + ads, with IAP to remove ads; premium + IAP; two versions (free with ads, paid without).

## Guidance
- Never put monetization ahead of the game; but decide the business model during design so mechanics embody it; IAP needs constant balancing and analytics services (pp. 285-286).
- Viral loops via social posting; under 5 percent of freemium players pay; average CPI about USD 1.50; "thousands of free users for dozens of payers" (p. 286).
- Lilypads sample: free version with banners, fewer levels, one undo; Gold upgrade removes ads and adds skins, levels, unlimited undo; stars -> virtual currency -> unlock levels, also purchasable (Ch. 12 pp. 303, 309-311).

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Assumes 2013 App Store era: USD 0.99 premium decline, banner/interstitial ads, early freemium (Clash of Clans, Bejeweled Blitz), virality via Facebook/Twitter, UA cheap (CPI USD 1.50). Dated as of 2026: CPI for casual puzzle is several times higher, rewarded video is the main ad format and is absent here, IAP is dominated by offers/passes/subscriptions (Battle Pass, VIP), premium share has shrunk further, attribution and privacy rules changed (ATT, GDPR), loot-box regulation exists.
- (b) A few cited market statistics (some now stale) plus authorial opinion; no retention/ARPDAU benchmarks.
- (c) Conflicts or is far thinner than [[concept-monetization-strategies]], [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]] (LTV vs CPI, payback), [[concept-iaa-ads-design]] (rewarded video, caps; book warns interstitials hurt retention, consistent but incomplete), [[concept-hybrid-models-and-ethics]] and [[concept-iap-packs-and-starter-pack]]. Its CPI/payer rate (under 5 percent) is rough compared with [[src-gd-fresher-f2p-monetization-training]] which models payer segments. Banner ads on puzzle screens are discouraged by modern practice.
- (d) Fit: low for tactics, medium for the principle "design the game around the business model from day one", which agrees with [[src-czerkawski-game-economy-design]].
