# Value of time vs money, and fairness to free players

Source: [[f2pdive-bycer-src]], 6.5, 9.3-9.4, 2.2.

## Core idea (6.5)
- Players differ in whether they value time or money. Money-over-time players grind slowly and refuse to pay; time-over-money players want content fast (limited daily play; collectors want everyone). The designer must cater to both as evenly as possible. Ignoring free players makes the game look P2W and greedy; ignoring time-poor ones makes them quit.
- Model: TF2. Payers buy any item at once, free players receive the same items over time through random drops, and skill still decides matches. Principle: free and paying players should reach the same point and play together; payers get there sooner.
- Two things to avoid: (1) paying that supersedes the need to play (hoops only free players jump through, or a game-breaking item only payers can get); (2) time cost wildly out of proportion to price: free players must always feel "close to something new". Documented case: For Honor math showed ~2.5 years of nonstop play to match what could be bought in year one; Ubisoft took press damage and rebalanced.
- Whale-only design is "highly unethical" and chases away a fan base; fans accept others spending more, not being locked out. Third-gen games are generous to free players because alternatives abound.

## Defining P2W (9.3, opinion with a nuanced scale)
- P2W is a spectrum, not a binary: a noticeable, gameplay-affecting gap between spenders and non-spenders. Pure "price tag on the best content" no longer ships; modern P2W is shortcut-versus-hoops (months vs instant). Gacha is subtler: everyone faces the same low odds, but more pulls give better odds; late game then demands characters free players can't afford. Walls between payer and free access (Marvel Strike Force character unlock chains) are unethical; paying should buy options, not power.
- "F2P challenge" streamers who progress months in a week know the game; do not use them as proof of fairness; skill has a ceiling.
- If an F2P game claims e-sport fairness, ask how much the top players spent at the start (9.3).

## Stance and applicability
(a) Competitive/PvP mobile and PC F2P with time-poor adults vs free-to-reach progression; whales as a design target he opposes. (b) The For Honor calculation and TF2 loop are documented; "what counts as P2W" is the author's judgement and he admits no game passes all PFD points. (c) Pushes against GD Fresher / Czerkawski's accepted reliance on whales, pay-to-skip and segmentation ([[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]] payer segments; [[concept-price-discrimination]] time-vs-money as a segmentation axis; [[concept-hybrid-models-and-ethics]]); Czerkawski's own three strategies ([[concept-monetization-strategies]]) treat "performance" monetization as an option, which Bycer would class as P2W. Zagal's "pay to skip" ([[concept-dark-game-design-patterns]]) is the closest ally. (d) Fit: medium-high. In a single-player match-3 + deco, "pay to skip" mostly buys boosters/levels, not competitive advantage, so PvP-fairness arguments weigh less; but the time/price proportionality check (how long to earn an item free vs buy) is a cheap, useful audit for our economy ([[concept-economy-building-blocks]]).
