# Content lifecycle and economy-steering techniques

Source: [[liveops-vasiuk-src]], ch. 9, pp. 104-115.

## Content utilisation lifecycle (pp. 107-109)
Stages: **Development, Introduction, Growth, Saturation, Decline**.
- Introduction: warm up existing players, early creators, re-engage lapsed.
- Growth: broad promotion to maximise ROI; give both time-rich (marathon events) and money-rich (paywall) players a path; **after the initial limited release, temporarily remove the content** to add exclusivity and avoid early saturation.
- Saturation: reuse; reintroduce when a large share of MAU has not touched it (e.g. a Christmas item returns 3-4 months later), move to **Permanent Sale** priced to the current economy, personalise promotions.
- Decline: discount, rebalance or remove. For newcomers old content is new, so content lifecycle must link to player lifecycle. Test new content on a subset first (skin lifecycle fig. 9.9).

## Techniques
- **Bundling and discounts** (pp. 109-110): discount elastic goods; align discounts with economy needs (discount items bought with a surplus currency to enlarge the sink); pure vs mixed bundles; filler items to tune apparent discount; bundles as **paywalls** to reduce demand for overpowered items. Ubisoft R6 Siege "no paywall" seasons as counter-example.
- **Variable currency exchange** (pp. 110-111): hard-to-soft conversion in fills of 10/50/100% of storage with better rates for larger fills; event-time better exchange rates (WoT 25 XP per gold to 35/40) to stimulate spending; discount hard-to-soft when hard currency is in surplus.
- **Resources exchange / multi-currency price** (pp. 111-112): price an asset in equivalent units, let players pay a mix of hard, several soft, boosters with per-resource caps (boosters up to 25%), first 30% of participants get an exclusive; Destiny 2 infusion as sink.
- **Auctions run by LiveOps** (pp. 112-114): English, turn-based, sealed, Dutch, buyout; to price new content and sink surplus; burden of continual desirable inventory.
- **Loot boxes** (p. 114): soft/hard pity, cosmetics only, show odds (law and trust); FIFA 09 Ultimate Team origin.
- **Single-game subscription** (pp. 114-115): introduce when players are deeply engaged; persistent benefits = access, multipliers, vanity; creates a sunk-cost baseline; ongoing content burden.

## Stance and applicability
- (a) Midcore content-heavy service with large catalogue (vehicles, skins), with enough surplus to need exchange rates and auctions; PC live service with a pricing/events team.
- (b) Mechanisms described with named examples but no measured outcomes.
- (c) Extends [[concept-live-ops-and-inflation]] (sinks, fusion, content spreadsheet) and [[concept-pacing-and-content-control]] (cycling content). **Disagrees with permanent availability instincts**: remove then re-run, whereas many deco games keep every item always available. Loot box and pity mechanics align with [[concept-battle-pass-vip-gacha]]; subscription with the monthly-card section there. Variable exchange rates and auctions are not in other wiki sources.
- (d) **Medium.** Directly usable: lifecycle-aware catalogue (rotating deco themes, return after 3-4 months), cosmetic-only random rewards with pity, event-time exchange boosts as a sink. Auctions, multi-currency pricing and single-game subscription are low fit for us.
