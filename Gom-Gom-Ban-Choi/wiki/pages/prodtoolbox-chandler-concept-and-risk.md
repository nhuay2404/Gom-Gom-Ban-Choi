# Concept definition, SWOT and risk management

Source: [[prodtoolbox-chandler-src]], Ch5 (pp. 81-94).

## Brainstorming (Ch5.2)
Focused purpose, a moderator who also takes notes, small relevant group, preparation (competitors, tech). Rules: no criticism, no discussion, no lulls (have prompting questions). Afterwards: group, prioritize, circulate notes within a day, assign research items of "no more than a couple of days". Sidebar (Brian Sowers): open-ended brainstorms at project start are "useless"; narrow sessions on one feature with a decision target work; index cards on a wall give structure.

## Initial concept checklist (Ch5.3)
Goals/mission statement (a litmus test for new ideas), **hook** (a "what if" question), **core game loop** (verbs the player repeats; Fortnite harvest-build-defend), genre, platform (mobile: short sessions, minutes not hours; touch is bad for accuracy/complex menus), target audience and **personas**, revenue model (F2P: core mechanics built around spending to progress faster; mass-market players try F2P, hardcore buy premium). Summarize into a one-page concept brief for stakeholder sign-off.

## Competitive and SWOT analysis (Ch5.4-5.5)
Competitive analysis: features, price, platform, model, launch date, sales, review scores; use it to forecast revenue. SWOT: strengths/weaknesses are internal and controllable, opportunities/threats external; write how to exploit or neutralize each; update through development.

## Concept treatment (Ch5.6)
20-50 pages: player experience of the **first five minutes** (challenges, rewards, learning curve, controls, verbs, social), the three C's (characters, controls, camera), accessibility, setting/story synopsis (2-3 paragraphs), concept art (days or weeks), audio design, technology (engine, licensing costs, tech risks). Paper changes are cheap; after prototype they get expensive.

## Risk management (Ch5.7, after McConnell)
List risks, estimate likelihood and impact, prioritize; 2x2 grid with four categories (high-probability/high-impact are categories 1-2 and need plans). Then control: management plan, execute, track. Examples: key staff leaving, vendor missing deliverable, pipeline not ready, licensor late approval. Whole team owns it; producer drives. Review each phase, in status reports and project reviews (Ch12.4.4, Ch14.4).

## Stance and applicability
(a) Concept phase of a funded premium/F2P title with a producer, lead designer/engineer/artist, and stakeholders approving a document; 2018 market framing.
(b) Practitioner experience; SWOT, personas, brainstorming rules are generic business-school tools. Risk method is McConnell's (software-engineering practitioner literature), not game-specific data.
(c) Conflicts: lean/data schools replace 20-50 page treatments with a rapid prototype plus cohort tests (see [[concept-ab-testing-monetization]], [[concept-analytics-and-kpis]]); Chandler's own Ch6 makes the same move in practice. The "revenue model shapes core mechanics" point agrees with [[concept-f2p-business-model-and-ltv]] and [[concept-monetization-strategies]], but she gives no pinch-point or economy guidance (see [[concept-core-loop-and-metagame]] for loop/meta structure). Persona building is more qualitative than analytics-first F2P practice.
(d) Fit: medium. The concept brief (goal, hook, loop, platform, audience, model), the "first five minutes" write-up and a ranked risk list are cheap and transferable. The 20-50 page treatment and SWOT form are overkill for a small team iterating on a known genre. Designer-only: high for the hook/loop/first-five-minutes parts, which are design work; low for risk grids.
